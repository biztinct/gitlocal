Rize HR Demo Cue Sheet

Five areas, in the order they tell a story: hiring someone, agreeing what they will do, telling the company things, time off and attendance, and training. Each act says what to click, what to say while you click it, and what the screen is proving.

Site payobook.com Company Payobook Vietnam JSC Running time about 35 minutes Prepared 18 Sep 2026

Read this before you book the call

These five areas are not on the Rize site yet. Hiring, Goals, Announcements and Training are installed on our own payobook.com only. The Rize site (rize.payobook.com) has the older time-off screens and nothing else from this list, so the demo has to run on payobook.com.

That means the people on screen are our demo cast with Vietnamese names, in a demo company. Say so once, at the start, and nobody minds: "This is our own demo company — your people and your numbers would sit in your own space." The India pay file you sent is the pay side, and that is the other cue sheet.

If Rize want to see these five areas on their own site with their own people, that is a separate install job on the Rize site — worth deciding before the call, because it changes what you can promise in the room.

Two minutes of setting up

Sign in on payobook.com as yourself. Check the company picker at the top says Payobook Vietnam JSC — the demo content lives there. Open a second browser tab, signed in as one of the demo staff, for the employee-side pages; you will switch to it three times.

Do not press Load demo data or Remove demo data under Settings. The second one takes the whole demo world away.

01

Hiring & recruitment

From the moment a manager says "I need somebody" to the moment that person has a start date and a contract.

Left rail → Lifecycle → Hiring 8 min
  1. Open Lifecycle in the left rail, then the Hiring tab.

    Let the top row of counters land first: Open for candidates, Waiting for a sign-off, Waiting on you, Candidates in play, Over budget, Interviews this week, Opinions overdue, Joined this month. This is the whole hiring desk on one screen.

  2. Click the Over budget counter.

    The list filters down to the requests that cost more than there is money for. This is the bit finance people sit up for: the request carries what it will cost, and the system checks it against the budget already in the system — within it, over it by this much, or nothing budgeted at all. Click the counter again to clear the filter.

  3. Press Raise a hiring request.

    Show the form — job title, how many people, which part of the business, what it will cost. Say that the request then travels the route the business published: the manager, the HR lead, the finance approver. Close it without saving; there is already a draft one (HR-2026-0055) to show.

  4. Open HR-2026-0056 from the Roles tab.

    The panel slides over and the path strip across the top shows where the role has got to — agreed, advert written, open for candidates, interviews, offer, joined. One line, no chasing emails.

  5. Point at the advert on that panel.

    The advert (DEMO Field Officer) is written once, agreed by name, and kept as versions — so nobody posts a job description that was never signed off. The buttons read Agree it and Advertise it.

  6. Switch to the Interviews tab.

    Nine interviews are booked. Each one sends a calendar invite. Everybody on the panel gets a private page of their own to write their opinion — nobody sees anybody else's first — and it is due within 24 working hours, with one automatic chase. The Opinions overdue counter is the one HR actually uses.

  7. Open the offer for DEMO Pham Quoc Cuong.

    This one is waiting on a manager, so you can show a real sign-off sitting in the queue. The offer carries its own lines (the money), a letter, and a page the candidate opens to say yes or no and upload the signed copy.

  8. Open a closed one — DEMO Nguyen Van An.

    This is the moment worth slowing down for. When the offer closed, the system made the employee record, the contract, a draft pay package, the joining checklist and their login. Nobody retyped anything.

  9. Second tab: open /my/refer.

    The staff side. Anybody in the company can put a friend forward from their own page, and it lands on this same board as a referral.

  10. Finish on Insights → Hiring.

    Average days to fill, where candidates came from, how many joined. There is an export to Excel button if they ask for their own numbers.

Say this

"Every one of these steps happens somewhere in your company today — in a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group and somebody's inbox. Here it is one board, and the request that started it all is still attached at the end."

What this act proves

  • A hiring request is checked against real budget, not approved on faith.
  • Sign-offs follow a published route, and you can see who is holding one up.
  • Interview opinions are private, timed and chased on their own.
  • Closing an offer creates the employee, the contract and the first-day list.
  • Staff refer people from their own page; recruiters see it instantly.

Careful

Run today's chasing is a real button — it sends the nightly chase mails now. Fine to show, don't press it unless you want mail to go.

The agency shown is called "Talent Partners" — a placeholder name we can change.

02

Goal setting

Everybody writes what they will do this year, their manager weighs it, HR locks it, and it is still there in November.

Left rail → People → Goals 7 min
  1. Open People, then the Goals tab.

    Five tabs run across it: Goal sheets, Monthly conversations, Reviews, Changes asked for, Scores. That is the year, left to right.

  2. Point at the goal year picker: DEMO Goal year 2026 is open, 2027 is still a draft, 2025 is closed.

    A goal year holds the dates, the half-way point, and how long somebody gets to write their goals.

  3. Hover Open the goal year for everybody — don't press it.

    One press opens a goal page for every person in the company, and it tells you how many it is about to open before it opens any. Seventeen sheets are already open on this year.

  4. Second tab: open /my/goals as a member of staff.

    This is the page an employee actually uses. Two to four goals, each with the key results that say whether it was met, and a weight. The weights must add up to 100 — the page will not let them send it otherwise. Then send to your manager.

  5. Back to the board. Open a sheet that is waiting.

    The manager sees the whole sheet and has two buttons: Agree and lock, or send it back with a note. Once it is locked, it cannot be quietly rewritten in March.

  6. Open the Changes asked for tab.

    Because things do change. After the lock, a change goes through a request with a reason — one was agreed here and one was turned down — and every version is kept.

  7. Open Monthly conversations.

    On the 25th of each month everyone is asked one short question about how each goal is going. It takes two minutes and it means the year-end conversation is not two people's memories.

  8. Open Scores and show the closed 2025 year.

    Key results get scored, the sheet gets a number, and the number becomes a word — Outstanding, Strong, Solid. One person on the demo finished at 4.2, Strong. The words and the cut-offs are yours to set.

  9. Finish on Home → Goals.

    The same information as a lane on the front page — goals waiting on you — so a manager sees it without going looking.

Say this

"The reason goals die in most companies is that they live in a file. Here they are a page that belongs to the person, a lock that belongs to HR, and a question that arrives every month on its own."

What this act proves

  • One press opens the year for the whole company, and warns first.
  • Weights must total 100 — the sheet enforces it, not a reviewer.
  • Locked means locked; changes are requested, agreed and kept.
  • Monthly check-ins arrive by themselves.
  • Year-end scoring turns into a band you choose the words for.

Careful

The demo year runs April–March with two weeks to write. If Rize run a different year, say that these are settings, not the way it works.

03

Company announcements

One calendar for everything the company is about to tell its people — and it sends itself.

Left rail → People → Announce 5 min
  1. Open People, then Announce.

    A month at a glance, one pill a day, coloured by where each announcement has got to. Eight are on this month.

  2. Point at two pills on the same day.

    This is the whole argument for a calendar instead of a list: the town-hall notice and the pay-day reminder both land in the same inbox an hour apart, and only a calendar shows you that before it happens.

  3. Open DEMO Pay day moves to the 25th — still to go out.

    Show the fields: what it is called, what it says, who gets it, when it goes out, and a poster or PDF that rides along with it.

  4. Open the audience picker and press Check who gets it.

    Everybody, or one department, or one job, or one country — and the count comes back before anything is sent. Nobody sends a Vietnam public-holiday notice to Singapore by accident.

  5. Press Put it on the calendar.

    That is the whole act of scheduling. It goes out on its own, at the minute it was booked for, to everyone in the audience with a work email.

  6. Open DEMO Monthly safety note.

    A recurring one — four have already gone out. Write it once, it comes round on its own.

  7. Open a sent one and show Who got it.

    One row per person. When somebody says "I never got told", there is an answer.

  8. Finish on Home → Coming up.

    What the company is about to be told, on the front page, for whoever is responsible.

Say this

"Announcements are usually written on the morning they go out, by whoever remembers. This is the same job done a fortnight early, with the audience counted before you press send."

What this act proves

  • A month view catches clashes a list hides.
  • Audience by everyone, department, job or country — counted first.
  • Scheduled sending, recurring notices, posters attached.
  • A delivery record per person.
  • A two-day window in which it can still be edited, and that edit is tracked.

Careful

Sending is on in this demo. Anything you schedule for a time inside the demo will actually go to demo addresses — harmless, but don't schedule one for two minutes' time and then forget it.

Birthday and anniversary cards are built but switched off. Mention them; don't go looking for them on screen.

04

Leave & attendance

Who is off, who approved it, who is late, and what the pay run should therefore be told.

Left rail → Workforce 8 min
  1. Open Workforce.

    The tabs across the top are a manager's week in order: Today, Schedule, Time, Time Off, Overtime, Trips, Approvals, Holidays, Field, Close.

  2. Start on Today.

    Who is in, who is out, who has not turned up. One screen, this morning.

  3. Open Time Off.

    Three things on one page: the approval queue, a month heat map of who is off by department, and everybody's balance. Approve one request from the queue, and show that refusing one makes you type a reason.

  4. Press Apply on behalf.

    HR books leave for somebody who phoned in. It is a normal request underneath — the same overlap and balance checks fire, nothing is smuggled past them.

  5. Say the escalation rule out loud while the queue is on screen.

    A manager gets two working days. The day after that they are chased. The day after that it goes to the HR lead. Nobody's leave sits unanswered because a manager is on a plane.

  6. Open Holidays.

    Thirteen public holidays are loaded. There is Add a public holiday for one, and Paste a year of holidays — paste a whole year in, one a line, and it reads them. India's list would go in in about a minute.

  7. Second tab: open /my/holidays.

    Every member of staff can see the holiday calendar for where they work, without asking HR.

  8. Open Approvals and pick the Attendance group.

    Missing punches, late arrivals, early departures. The system never invents a punch: it raises the exception, somebody requests a correction, somebody approves it, and only then does the record change. That is what makes attendance safe to pay from.

  9. Open Field.

    Staff who work away from a desk check in from their phone with a photo and a location. For a farming business with people in the field, this is usually the moment they lean in. There is a phone page at /field if you want to show it on a handset.

  10. Mention the two rails without clicking them.

    Back-dated sick leave is allowed but tells HR. Leave in a month that has already been paid is locked, so nobody quietly changes a paid month.

Say this

"Leave and attendance only matter because they change what someone is paid. So everything here is either approved by a person or refused — nothing is adjusted in the background, and once a month is paid it locks."

What this act proves

  • Approve or refuse in one click, with a reason required on refusal.
  • Heat map by department; balances for everybody on one board.
  • Requests escalate on their own: manager, chase, HR lead.
  • Public holidays pasted in a year at a time, visible to staff.
  • Attendance corrections are requested and approved, never invented.
  • Field staff check in from a phone with a photo.

Careful

Only one person is set up as field staff on the demo, so the map is quiet. Say it is one agronomist, not a bug.

Carry-forward caps are set to zero, which means "not watched". If they ask, that is a setting per leave type.

05

Learning & development

Put people through a course, let them take the test, hand them the certificate — and pay back what they spent.

Left rail → Learn → Training 7 min
  1. Open Learn, then Training.

    The counters say it in one line: how many courses, how many people are on one, not started, finished, passed the test, the pass rate, what is past its date and what is due in the next week.

  2. Open the course DEMO Agronomist basics.

    A course is lessons — a video, a document, a written page, a picture, a short question — and a test at the end.

  3. Press Put people on it.

    One person, or a whole department, or everybody doing one job. Set the date it is due by.

  4. Second tab: open /my/training as the demo learner.

    This is the page the employee sees, and it is deliberately small: the courses they are on, how far through each one, what is left, and whether the test is still locked. No catalogue to get lost in. Open a lesson, press Mark as done, watch the progress bar move.

  5. Show the test and the certificate on that page.

    The test unlocks when the lessons are done. Pass it and the certificate is issued and filed against the person — so an auditor asking "prove this person was trained" takes one click, not one afternoon.

  6. Back to the board: Assignments.

    Seven on the demo, in every state including one that was missed. Reminders go three days before, one day before, on the day, then every three days. The manager is told two days after it is late; the HR lead five days after.

  7. Open More time.

    Somebody genuinely could not finish — they ask for more time from their own page, and a manager agrees it. It is a request with a name on it, not a quiet extension.

  8. Open Comes round again and Day one.

    Yearly safety training that reappears on its own, and the courses a new joiner starts on their first day — those sit on the joining checklist the offer created back in act one.

  9. Open Claims.

    Somebody paid for a course themselves. They upload the receipt and the certificate, it goes for sign-off against a yearly allowance, and once agreed it becomes an item the pay team queues into a pay run. It never writes itself into a payslip — a person still puts it on the run.

  10. Finish on Insights → Training.

    Who has been trained in what, what it cost, what is overdue — with an Excel export.

Say this

"Training usually lives in three places: a folder of videos, a spreadsheet of who watched them, and somebody's memory of who passed. This is the same three things in one place, and the employee can see their own list."

What this act proves

  • Courses, tests and certificates all on our own pages.
  • Enrol a department or a job in one action, with a due date.
  • Reminders and escalation run themselves.
  • Compliance training comes round yearly; joiners start on day one.
  • Course costs are claimed, approved against an allowance, and reach the pay run through a person.

Careful

The yearly training allowance on screen is a demo figure, not a recommendation.

There is one course on the demo. If they ask "is that all", say the stock sample courses were deliberately removed so nothing fake is on screen.

06

Closing

Two minutes. Land the three things that are true across all five areas.

Anywhere · or Home 2 min
  1. One inbox for every sign-off.

    The hiring request, the offer, the goal lock, the extra time on a course, the training claim, the leave — every one of them runs on the same approval engine, and every one of them shows up in one approvals list. You configure who signs off what once.

  2. The employee side is real, not an afterthought.

    Refer a friend, write your goals, do your training, see the holidays, check in from the field — all from pages the employee can reach on a phone.

  3. It ends in the pay run.

    A closed offer becomes a contract and a pay package. A training claim becomes something the pay team puts on a run. Attendance and leave decide what is paid. That is the difference between an HR system and a set of forms.

  4. Offer the next step.

    The honest one: put these five areas on the Rize site with Rize's own people, and run the same walk-through on their data.

If they ask

  • Is it in other languages? Every screen here is English and Vietnamese; another language is a translation job, not a rebuild.
  • Dark mode? Not yet on these screens — they render light. Everything is readable; it is on the list.
  • Can we have our own approvers? Yes — that is a settings screen, per company.
  • Can staff see all this on a phone? The employee pages, yes. The HR boards are built for a laptop.
