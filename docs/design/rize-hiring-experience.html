Rize · Hiring · experience design

Hiring Experience Blueprint

The one design every phase of the Rize Hiring rebuild builds to. A recruiter who has never seen the product should open it, understand the board in ten seconds, and get through a whole hiring cycle without a training session. The screens below are drawn to scale with demo data so you can judge the feel before anything is coded.

Prepared 30 September 2026Follows the gap register (G-01 … G-58) and your three rulings of todayBuilt on the Payobook indigo kit; Lucide icons; light and dark

Seven promises the design keeps

01

One door, one room

"Hiring" opens on your roles. Open a role and you are on its board. Everything else (candidate, request, job description, adverts, set-up) opens from where you are, never from a menu tree.

02

The screen says what to do next

Every role card and every candidate carries one plain sentence and one button: "Two opinions are in, one is late. Remind Minh." Nothing is left for the reader to work out.

03

Rize's words, everywhere

Applications received, Shortlist, Recruiter review, Discussion 1, CV reject. The talent lead's description of each stage shows under its column name, so a stand-in recruiter reads the process off the board.

04

Move things with your hands

Drag a card. Tick three and move them together. Edit a stage name by clicking it. No "Change status" dialog, no dropdown-then-update.

05

Nothing blocks; everything undoes

Any card to any column, no sign-off in the way. A move shows a five-second "Undo". Reasons are offered, never demanded. The one exception, sending an offer, explains itself when it cannot.

06

Empty states teach

A column with nobody in it says what belongs there. A new role shows the three things to do before publishing. A first-time visitor gets three hints, once.

07

Private until shared

A candidate belongs to the recruiter. Sharing with a hiring manager or a panel is one explicit control that says exactly what they will see. Money and recruiter notes carry a lock.

1
Header chips answer the four questions a stand-in asks: which team, how many seats, is there an agreed request, where is it advertised. Each chip opens the matching part of Details.
2
The quiet numbers line (your approved pattern): grey at zero, colour only when someone must act. Press a figure to narrow the board; press it again to clear.
3
Column headers carry the count, a "~N days here" caption from real stage timings, and on hover the talent lead's one-line meaning of the stage. Hidden stages for this role are one click away for the talent lead ("Show a hidden stage").
4
Cards show name, when they applied and from where, and at most two chips. A coloured left edge means: green shortlisted, amber waiting on somebody, rose a problem. Hover shows Advance, Not this time and Open.
5
Drag anywhere. The target column lights up; the card lands; a toast names what happened and offers Undo for five seconds. Automations that fired (like the Calendly email) are named in the toast, so nothing happens silently.
6
Multi-select by ticking cards (or shift-click a range). A floating bar moves, emails or closes them together. Keyboard: arrows move focus, Enter opens, M moves, Esc clears.
7
Closed rail keeps the outcome stages honest without stealing width. Dropping on it opens a small popover: which outcome, an optional reason, and a tick to send the "not this time" email in the candidate's language.
8
Empty columns explain themselves in one sentence. The Offer column also states the one hard rule in context, so the recruiter learns it before hitting it.

The candidate drawer

Opening a card slides a drawer over the board; the board stays visible behind it so the recruiter never loses their place. The drawer is one scroll, no tabs: the next step first, then the story of this person top to bottom.

Requests without friction phase 3

The manager's pageThe email link opens one page, no login hunt: the role at the top, the questions in order, saving as they type, a progress line ("6 of 9 answered"). Budget is a plain money field with a live, gentle note: "This is above the department's budget. The CEO, Head of HR and Finance will be told when you submit. Nothing stops."
Reminders the recruiter controlsOn the role card: "Waiting on Budi · reminded 2 days ago · Remind now · every 2 days". After 3 business days the talent lead is told automatically.
Sign-offs record, never blockManager, HR and Finance get a note and a one-click "Noted / Agreed". The role does not wait. The request's chip says "Request agreed" only when the manager and HR have pressed it; until then the Offer column explains that sending an offer will wait.
Job descriptionLives on the role's Details tab as a versioned text with "Share draft with Budi for input". Edits flow to the advert with a "Republish" nudge.

Scorecards that people actually fill in phase 5

The interviewer's pageOne private link, no login. The candidate's name, the round, the CV if shared. Then the scorecard the talent lead designed for this role and round: free-text areas, a five-star rating, and four big decision buttons Yes / Maybe / No / Hold. A line at the top: "Your answers stay hidden from the other panellists until everyone is in."
The recruiter's viewPer candidate, a grid: rows are panellists, columns are the scorecard's parts, with the decision as a coloured chip. "Enter it for them" opens the same form with a note "entered by An from Slack, 3 Oct" that stays on the record.
RemindersA chase after 24 working hours, then daily until done, each one naming the candidate and the round; the talent lead is told when a panellist is two days late. Moving the candidate on never stops a late scorecard from being submitted.
Phone screenMoving a card into Recruiter review sends the recruiter's own Calendly link automatically; the card shows "Calendly sent · slot picked Thu 10:00" once the calendar hold appears. The transcript slot on the interview is visible to recruiters and the talent lead only.

From offer to joined phase 6

Offer"Send offer" is the one button that can refuse, and when it does it says why in a sentence with the fix: "The request for this role is not agreed yet. Ask Budi and Nithya to press Agree." Money on the offer shows a lock naming who can see it.
Post-offerA signed offer moves the card to Post-offer with a countdown: "Joining Mon 3 Nov · 9 days". Under it, the recruiter's pre-joining list: ask Budi to name a buddy · ask Lan for laptop preferences · set up a coffee chat. Each is one button that sends the right email or link.
The week beforeRecruiter, hiring manager and HR get one email: "Lan joins on Monday. Still on? · Date changed · Did not join". Each answer is a button that does the right thing.
Joined"Confirm they joined" on the day creates the employee, contract and login and hands over to HR's onboarding. "Did not join" records an offer drop with a reason. Hiring numbers then show offered → accepted → joined per market.

Rules that bind every screen

Motion with purposeCards lift on drag and settle on drop (150 ms). Columns highlight when a card is over them. Count pills tick. Drawers slide from the right. Nothing bounces, nothing loops. Reduced-motion users get instant changes.
Undo, not confirmReversible actions (moves, shares, note edits, reminders) show a toast with Undo for five seconds. Confirmations are only for the irreversible: sending an offer, confirming a joining, purging data.
Every state designedLoading shows skeleton cards in the columns, not a spinner. Empty columns and empty boards teach. Errors say what went wrong and what to press. A huge board (500 cards) shows the first 30 per column with "Show 40 more".
First visitThree hints, shown once and dismissable: "Drag a card to move someone", "Press a number to narrow the board", "Everything you move can be undone". Hover on any column name shows the talent lead's meaning of that stage.
KeyboardArrow keys move between cards, Enter opens, M moves, E emails, N notes, Esc closes; ⌘K reaches any role or person. The floating bar appears after the first tick.
Plain wordsNo internal vocabulary on screen. Rize's stage names. "Not this time" for reject, "Waiting on Minh" not "pending approval", "Agreed" not "validated". The word "Odoo" never appears.
Light and darkBoth themes designed, both checked in the browser before a phase is reported done.

What each phase builds from this blueprint

Phase 1

Board, stages, the non-blocking rule

Hiring home, the role board with drag-and-drop, closed rail, quiet numbers, drawer with Next box and timeline, Rize's stage set with presets and per-role visibility, role tiers renamed, gates removed, stock menus hidden, first-visit hints.

Phase 2

Application forms

The builder, templates, required files, expected pay, consent, three languages, duplicate flag.

Phase 3

Requests, budget, the offer rule

Open a role directly, Ask a manager, the manager's page, non-blocking sign-offs, budget flag, JD sharing, the explicit offer rule.

Phase 4

Privacy and resume bank

Share with…, locks on money and notes, private recruiter notes, Resume bank tab, consent and retention.

Phase 5

Interviews and scorecards

Scorecard templates per role and round, interviewer page, proxy entry, reminders, Calendly rule, Google Calendar + Meet, transcript slot, side-by-side debrief.

Phase 6

Joining and numbers

Post-offer, countdown, pre-joining list, week-before email, Confirm they joined, offer-drop funnel, market filters, ageing.

Phase 7

Channels and agencies

Publish-to panel with tracked links, agency portal, 6-month cooling rule.

Phase 8

Communications

Language versions of every candidate email, the automations list, notification deltas.
