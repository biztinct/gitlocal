Findings and proposal

Who gets the portal, who gets the backend

An honest look at how employee logins work in Payobook today, what the best HR products do, and a strategy for general employees versus staff.

Prepared 15 Oct 2026For the product ownerSources: live code of this build, vendor help centres

Short answer

  1. No, there is no "portal only" role today. Every real employee login is a full account. The system has no role called Employee or Self-service.
  2. The nine dots at the top left of the portal are the backend shortcut, and every full account sees them, plain employees included. One click opens the backend Home. The "Apps" link in the account menu and typing the address are the other two doors. Inside the backend the app switcher is already hidden for a plain employee (they only have one app).
  3. The portal already has most of what a general employee needs. Payslips, leave, schedule, documents, profile changes, training, goals, recognition, resignation, onboarding, referrals are all there. The gaps are tickets, clock in and out, expenses, announcements and one "my requests" list.
  4. The market pattern is clear: one product, one login, one brand. Employees get a "My" world with their own records only. Managers get a "My team" slice. Admin tools sit behind a separate role, and an admin who is also an employee switches between the two views.
  5. Proposal: two tiers. Employee = a portal account, the default for everyone hired, never sees the backend. Staff = a full account with roles, as today. Built in four phases, the first of which locks the door this week.

1. What happens today

The system has two account types, exactly like the products in the market comparison below. A full account opens the backend (the admin web app). A portal account can only open the portal pages under "My Payobook" and is physically unable to load the backend. The problem is not that the second type is missing. It is that employees are never given it.

What a plain employee with a full account sees in the backend

The system already tries to keep them out of harm's way. It recognises an "employee only" person (an employee record, no payroll or hiring rights, not an admin) and does four things: lands them on My Payobook after sign-in, shows only the Payroll app so the nine dots vanish, hides the Pulse tab on Home, and skips the first-login welcome card. That is a courtesy, not a wall. Here are the doors that remain open:

1

The purple corner with the nine dots on every portal page opens the backend Home.

It is shown to every full account. Home then shows Approvals, the recognition Wall and Announcements, plus a left rail with Home, Learn and a locked Settings.
2

The "Apps" item in the account menu (top right of the portal) does the same.

Same condition: any full account.
3

Typing the backend address by hand works. The sign-in redirect sends them to My Payobook only when they have no destination in mind.

Hidden menus hide buttons, not data. Any record a full account is allowed to read (for example their own leave requests in the native leave screen) can still be opened by address.
4

Every full account automatically carries two extra rights: the approvals inbox and the AI assistant.

So every employee today is, technically, an AI assistant user and an approvals user, whether or not they ever use either.
5

Single sign-on through the connected HR system skips the "send employees to My Payobook" rule entirely and lands them in the backend.

Only matters for tenants using that sign-on.
What this means for you. Today's "employee only" behaviour is a nice landing, not a security boundary. A curious employee can reach the backend in two clicks and can see whatever the native screens allow a full account to see. Nothing dangerous (payroll screens are gated by role), but it is a product the employee was never meant to see, and it looks like an admin tool.

Six patterns worth copying

One product, one login

The employee never sees a second "admin" product. Payobook's portal and backend are two faces of one product; the employee should only ever meet one.

"My" means mine only

Own payslips, own leave, own documents. Company-wide data is a separate right, never a side effect of having a login.

Propose, don't edit

Personio's "Propose" is our profile change request. The employee asks, HR approves, the record is written once. We already have this.

Managers get "My team", not the admin tool

Approvals and a team view are the only manager features in the employee-facing app. Everything else is staff work.

Help lives inside self-service

Workday Help cases, Personio Conversations, Rippling's requests: the employee asks a question where they already are, and it lands on HR's desk.

Phone first for employees

Employment Hero and BambooHR lead with the mobile app for employees. Payslip, leave, clock in: three taps.

3. The strategy: two tiers

Match the market. Give every person one of two account types, and make the choice obvious on the Access screen.

Employee

Everyone by default. Warehouse staff, drivers, office staff, anyone who is paid.
  • A portal account. Cannot load the backend at all, not by click, not by address.
  • Lives in My Payobook: payslips, leave, schedule, documents, profile changes, training, goals, recognition, onboarding, referrals.
  • Gains: help tickets, clock in and out, expenses, announcements, one "My requests" list.
  • No roles to manage. "Employee" is the role.

Team lead

Supervisors who approve leave, timesheets and fixes for their people.
  • Phase 1 to 3: a full account with the approvals and lifecycle "team" roles only. They land on My Payobook and can open the backend Home for approvals.
  • Later (optional): a "My team" page on the portal, so most team leads can drop to an Employee account too.

Staff

HR, payroll, finance, recruiters, admins. The people who run the product.
  • A full account with role bundles from the Access screen, exactly as today.
  • They also have an employee record, so they see their own payslips in My Payobook, and switch to the backend through the purple corner. That is the Gusto "switch" pattern and it already works.

What changes on the Access screen

The People lens gets a single switch on each person: Employee (portal only) or Staff (full account). New hires default to Employee. Promoting someone to Staff flips the switch and then roles can be handed out. Demoting does the reverse and strips the roles. Nothing else about the Access screen changes.

4. Three ways to get there

Option A

Lock the door, keep full accounts

  • Block the backend address for employee-only people and send them to My Payobook.
  • Hide the purple corner and the "Apps" link for them.
  • Fix the single sign-on gap.

Size: small, a few days. No change to how logins are created.

A fence, not a wall. The account can still read whatever a full account can read. It is the right first step but a poor final state, and it still carries the AI and approvals rights.
Recommended
Option B

Two tiers: portal accounts for employees

  • Make every "My Payobook" page work for a portal account (five places write as the signed-in person and currently refuse a portal account; the home page's employee look; finding the employee for auto-made logins).
  • Add "Employee / Staff" to the Access screen with promote and demote.
  • New hires get Employee by default. Convert existing employee-only accounts in one sweep.
  • Close the portal feature gaps so no employee ever needs the backend.

Size: medium. Four phases, roughly two weeks of build in total plus the new portal features.

A real wall, the market pattern, lighter on the server (portal accounts never load the backend), and it makes the Access screen honest about who is who.
Option C

Portal accounts, no fence first

  • As B, but skip the quick lock in Option A and go straight to converting accounts.

Size: as B minus a few days.

Saves a few days but leaves the door open for the weeks until conversion. Not worth it; the fence is cheap and its work is reused.
Recommendation: Option B, starting with Option A's fence as Phase 1. The fence is small, lands this week, and every piece of it (who counts as employee-only, where they are sent) is reused when portal accounts take over.

6. Phases

Phase 1Fence · days

Lock the backend for employee-only people today.

  • Backend address sends them to My Payobook. The nine-dots corner and the "Apps" link are not drawn for them. Single sign-on lands on the same locked address, so it needs no change.
  • Team leads keep the backend: anyone with a role, a direct report or a decision waiting on them is never locked out.
Phase 2Portal accounts · 1 week

Make My Payobook complete for a portal account.

  • The five refusals above, the employee look of the home page, finding the employee for auto-made logins.
  • Employee / Staff switch on the Access screen with promote and demote. New hires default to Employee.
  • Demo logins: the ten employee demos become portal accounts so the demo shows the real thing.
  • The two automatic rights (AI assistant, approvals) stop reaching employees on their own, because a portal account never carries them.
Phase 3Employee features · 1 to 2 weeks

Close the gaps that force an employee into the backend.

  • "Ask HR" tickets, "My requests", clock in and out on the portal, Announcements on the portal home.
Phase 4Switch-over · days

Convert existing employee-only accounts in one sweep, tenant by tenant, with a rollback list.

  • Expenses, business trips and timesheet entry follow as separate small phases when a tenant needs them.

7. Decisions I need from you

1. Which way?

Two tiers with the fence first (recommended), the fence only, or portal accounts without the fence.

2. Team leads

Keep them on full accounts for approvals (recommended for now), or build "My team" on the portal in Phase 3 so they can be Employee accounts too.

3. "Ask HR"

In scope for Phase 3, or park it. There is no ticketing anywhere in the product today, so this is new ground rather than a port.
