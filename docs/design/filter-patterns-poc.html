One filter for every list in Payobook

Three ways to filter a list, each built as a working mock of the Employees screen on the same sample of 140 people, plus the upgraded panel built after the owner picked Option B. Click around in each one. The scoring and the final recommendation are at the bottom.

What the Employees screen does today

The filter block is a panel of buttons that grows with the number of departments. It is the tallest thing on the page and it only knows two questions.

The problems, in order

  • Height. With 32 departments the filter panel is about 380 px tall. On a laptop that is roughly 40% of the screen before the first employee appears.
  • Only two things to filter on. Join date and department. No country, status, contract type, pay band, manager, work location, payroll-readiness, or contract end date.
  • One choice at a time. You cannot pick two departments, or a department plus a country.
  • Every screen builds its own. Employees, Contracts, Pay structures and Hiring each have their own chip rows with different rules. A filter learned on one screen does not carry to the next.
  • Nothing is saved. Each visit starts from "All time, all departments".

The three options, live

Each tab is a working mock. The roster, the numbers and the filter engine are shared; only the way you reach the filters changes. Try the same job in each: show Running employees in Vietnam and Indonesia who joined this year. The first tab is the upgraded panel; the other three are the original options for comparison.

Try: type "hanoi" in the panel's find box and see Work location and Department open with just the matching values. Hover a value and press "Only". Click a chip above the list to jump to its section. Hover a section title and pin it. Press "Hide filters" and notice the chips stay. Change something and press "Save view".

Kept from B

  • Every field in view, nothing to discover.
  • A live count beside every value, recomputed against the other filters. Values at zero fade, they never disappear.
  • Tick to narrow, in any order. Sections collapse and remember.
  • The layout payroll and HR suites use (Workday reports, ADP, Rippling's filter rail).

Borrowed from A

  • Saved views as tabs above the bar, with "Save view", "Reset" and a dot when the view has unsaved changes. These replace today's hard-coded buttons.
  • Chips above the list show what is applied; clicking one jumps to its section.
  • Find a filter at the top of the panel searches field names and values at once ("hanoi", "manager", "probation").
  • Pinned sections stay at the top; the rest sit under "More filters", collapsed.
  • Hide filters gives the list the full width while the chips keep the state readable.

Still costs, and how it is softened

  • About 272 px of width while open. Hidden state is remembered per screen, and the chips mean nothing is lost.
  • On a narrow window or phone the same panel slides in as a drawer (shrink this window to see it).
  • Every tick re-counts the sections on the server. The counts are cached per screen and only the open sections are counted.
  • Boards that already use the width (kanban with many columns) start with the panel hidden.
Try: click "Add filter", type "cou" and pick Country. Then click the Department chip to change it. Then press "Save view" to keep what you built as a tab.

Why it works

  • Takes one line. Everything else is the list.
  • Any number of fields, any number of values, no extra height.
  • The applied filters are always readable as a sentence of chips.
  • Saved views replace today's hard-coded status buttons and date rows.
  • Works unchanged on a list, a kanban board or a card grid, and on a phone.
  • This is what people already know from Linear, Notion, Shopify, Stripe.

Where it costs

  • You do not see every possible value until you open a pill.
  • Two clicks for a field that is not pinned (Add filter → field → value).
  • Needs a small server call per pill to fetch the values and counts.

Details that make it premium

  • Type-to-find in every picker; keyboard up/down/enter.
  • Counts next to each value, recalculated against the other filters.
  • Chips are clickable to edit, not just removable.
  • "Clear all" undoes with one click; the view tab shows a dot when it has unsaved changes.
  • The link in the address bar carries the filters, so a view can be shared.
Try: tick two countries and watch every other count change. Press "Hide filters" to give the list the whole width back. Open "Department" and use "Show all".

Why it works

  • Everything is visible at once; nothing to discover.
  • Counts beside every value answer "how many?" before you click.
  • Great for exploring when you do not know what you are looking for.

Where it costs

  • Spends about 270 px of width. Payobook already has a main rail and a sub-rail on the left; this makes a third column.
  • Long value lists (32 departments, 50 managers) need "show more" and a search inside the panel anyway.
  • On a phone it has to become a drawer, so you build two patterns.
  • Every tick re-counts every section on the server. Fine at 140 rows, costly at 4,500.
  • Awkward on kanban boards and dashboards that already use the width.

Best fit

  • Catalogue-style screens: pay components, report catalogue, learning library.
  • Less suited to day-to-day work lists that are scanned fast.

Why it works

  • The quietest bar of the three. The list gets all the room.
  • Room for every field, grouped, with explanations.
  • "Match all / match any" and a live "Show N employees" button make complex filters safe.
  • Same component on desktop and phone.

Where it costs

  • Every filter is behind a click. Changing one value is open → change → apply.
  • The drawer covers the list, so you cannot see the effect while choosing (only the count).
  • Feels like "settings", not like working with the list.

Best fit

  • The advanced step behind Option A for the rare complex question (Insights, Explorer, audit lists).
  • Not the daily driver.

Scoring and recommendation

Scored on what matters for a work list that someone opens many times a day. Five dots is best. The bar still wins on raw space; the upgraded panel wins on finding things and matches what payroll suites do, which is why it is now the recommendation.

What mattersB+ · Panel upgradedA · BarB · FacetsC · Drawer
B+
Recommendation, updated after the owner chose B

Facet panel on the left, upgraded with the best of A

  1. It is the panel you chose, kept whole. Every field visible, a live count beside every value, tick to narrow. This matches the payroll and HR suites people compare us with.
  2. Saved views fix the biggest gap. "Not payroll-ready", "Expiring soon", "Joined this month" become tabs anyone can add or rename. Product defaults ship with each screen.
  3. Find a filter makes 11 fields feel like 3. Typing "hanoi" opens the two sections that hold it. Typing "manager" jumps to the field. No scrolling through sections.
  4. Chips and pins keep it fast. Chips above the list show what is on and jump to the section. Pinned sections stay on top; everything else waits under "More filters".
  5. Hiding it costs nothing. One click gives the list the full width; the chips stay, the state stays, and the choice is remembered per screen.
