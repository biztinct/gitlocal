Decisions waiting for you

  1. Push the work.More than 1,600 commits sit on the branch, unpushed — this wave and everything before it.
  2. Move the HR-lead seat off your account.On the Vietnam company that seat is still you, so late leave, late courses, claims and goal locks all land in your inbox.
  3. The award sitting on a pay run.1,200,000 ₫ was queued onto the draft June retail demo run to prove the money path. Pay it with the run or take it off.
  4. Real figures and dates.The training allowance, the goal-year dates, the check-in day and the minimum-months rules, the carry-forward caps, the 2026/27 holiday dates, who is field staff.
  5. Wording.The two offer letters, the agency name "Talent Partners", the payslip line "Training reimbursement", the goal band names.
  6. Small clean-ups.Delete the leftover sample test "MyCompany Vendor Certification"; two small add-ons arrived automatically with the test engine; ten internal to-do emails across the product fail for want of a sender address.
  7. Two product-wide jobs, not part of this wave.Dark mode looks like light mode on every cockpit screen; and the older wave-1 modules carry a weakness where a failed follow-up email can undo an approval's own status change. The new modules are built without it.
  8. Announcements.Name who looks after announcements in each company (today it is whoever holds HR lead), decide whether they need a sign-off first, and pick the day to switch the birthday cards on — about 32 people a day.

One money rule, one demo rule

A training claim reaches a payslip only the way a bonus does: agreeing it raises an award, and the pay team queues that award into a run from the Awards panel after a preview. Nothing writes to a payslip directly.

Nothing on the demo says the customer's name any more. Every demo record starts with DEMO and sits on one register in Settings → Demo data. The only places the name survives are the customer's real tenant record, its backups and the monitoring alerts about it — those have to keep it.
