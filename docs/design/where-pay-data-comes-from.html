Design proposal · Payobook

Where Pay Data Comes From

Five changes with one idea behind them: you should be able to see, choose and trust every source that feeds a pay run. Each change below exists because a real problem happened — the problem is shown next to the fix.

Only real fields on the board Sync on your schedule Turn sources on or off You set who wins Screens follow your choices
Why

The problems this ends

All four happened on your live setup. None of them announced itself — each one was found only after the numbers looked wrong.

Invented fields

The mapping board listed 50 field names the connected system never sends — with made-up examples like “Nguyen Van An” and “18,500,000”, and Indian tax fields on a Vietnamese payroll. They came from a built-in list, not from your data.

Three empty pay runs

Because “Salary” looked real (fake example beside it), it got mapped instead of the field that actually arrives. Three pay runs computed on nothing before the cause was found.

Corrections undone

The connected system always wins and always writes into Payobook. Fix a value by spreadsheet and the next sync quietly puts the old one back. There was no way to say “Payobook is the source of truth.”

One rigid clock

The sync runs monthly, on the 5th, at a time nobody chose — the same for every client, buried where nobody looks, with no way to say “every Monday morning” or “last day of the month”.

An honest field list

The board will only show two kinds of fields: ones the connected system’s own definition declares, and ones that have actually arrived in real data — with real example values taken from your data, refreshed on every sync. The invented list is deleted.

2

Sync on your schedule

Each connected system gets its own schedule, set right on its screen — daily, weekly on a day you pick, monthly on a date, or the last day of the month, at a time on your clock, not the server’s. Try it:

A daily or weekly run fetches this month so far — and during the first week of a new month it also re-fetches last month, so late corrections still arrive before you close last month’s pay run.

3·4

You decide the sources — and who wins

Every scheme gets a Sources card: three sources you can switch on or off and put in order. The order decides two things at once — whose value a pay run uses, and who is allowed to update Payobook’s stored data. A lower source may fill an empty box, but it can never overwrite what a higher source owns. This demo is live — reorder and watch the outcome change:

5

Screens follow your choices

Switch a source off and every place that offered it steps aside — not just hidden on screen, but refused by the system too, so nothing can slip in through a side door.

✓

What keeps this safe

Nothing changes until you change it

Every scheme starts with today’s exact behaviour: all three sources on, connected system first. The new controls change nothing until you touch them.

One rulebook, everywhere

Runs with a file, runs without a file, and record updates all follow the same order. The same month can never again give two different answers depending on how it was run.

Warned before you break something

Switching off a source that components currently rely on shows exactly how many, and what they’ll fall back to — before you save.

Proven before it ships

Twelve numbered checks — including “old behaviour unchanged by default” — run green before deployment to all four databases, then verified live on screen with screenshots.

Plan

How it gets built

1 · Honest field list

Delete the invented fields, capture real examples from every sync, add the “last seen” story to the board.

2 · Schedule editor

Per-system schedule on the connection screen, in your timezone, with a plain sentence showing the next run.

3 · The source engine

Teach the pay-run calculation and the record-update machinery to obey your on/off switches and order — including “never overwrite, only fill empty”.

4 · The visible layer

The Sources card on scheme settings, the wizard that reshapes itself, the gated tiles and tabs — then live checks on your site, with screenshots.
