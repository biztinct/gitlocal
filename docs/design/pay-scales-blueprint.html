PART A

Your brief, and the three places I push back

Your instinct is right in the parts that matter: a company should be able to say "a store associate is paid this" once, and have that fact flow into every new contract and every rise. Three details of the brief would cause real harm in a payroll product, and every serious tool on the market avoids them. Here they are side by side.

Scale and band are two different tools, and you need both

A band is a range (lowest to highest) used for judgement: is this person paid reasonably for the job? Payobook shipped bands last week. A scale is a point (grade 3, step 2 = this exact amount) used as a rule: this is what the job pays. Every large employer in this region runs on a scale; every start-up runs on bands; a group with both a factory and a head office has both. The design keeps them as two tabs that know about each other: a scale's steps sit inside a band, and the band picture shows the steps as ticks.

One finding worth acting on regardless of this design

The only minimum-wage check in the product uses Vietnam's 2022 figures. Two decrees have passed since (July 2024 and January 2026). A contract at ₫5,000,000 in Ho Chi Minh City passes the check today and is illegal. Part E fixes this properly; if you want, the figures alone can be corrected in an afternoon before anything else.

PART C

The model: five ideas that carry everything

Five plain ideas. Every screen in Part D is a view of one or two of them.

1 · Job ladder

Job family → grade → step. A grade is a rung (Store associate, Senior associate, Shift lead). A step is a position on the rung, usually earned by time. Steps are optional: a scale can be grades only.

2 · Pay scale

The table of amounts: one per grade and step, in one currency, for one country, for one or more companies, in force from a date. Two ways to write it: an amount, or a coefficient times a base rate (the Vietnamese "ngạch, bậc" style). Every past version is kept.

3 · Pay policy

The sentences that say how the scale is used: where joiners start, who follows the scale, when steps move, what happens above the top, how rounding works, which floor applies, what the employee may see. Written and read as sentences, tested against real people before saving.

4 · Scale status on every contract

On scale (grade and step known, pay equals the scale), Pinned (deliberately different, with a reason), Drifted (was on scale, now differs, needs a decision). Nothing is ever pulled back without someone choosing to.

5 · Revision

A dated change to the scale. Editing a scale never touches anyone's pay directly; it produces a revision, and the revision becomes a change set: who follows, what it costs through the real payroll, who approves, apply, arrears if backdated, letters. One trail per person, shared with reviews and one-off changes.

How the pieces talk to each other

  1. A job points at a grade

    Every job in the organisation is linked to one grade of one scale (it already links to a band). A person's grade is their job's grade unless someone sets it differently on the contract.

  2. A new contract reads the scale

    Job → grade → the policy's joiner rule → step → amount. The hire screen shows the amount and the sentence that produced it. Editing the amount pins the person with a reason, or keeps them on scale if the amount still equals a step.

  3. The scale changes, the revision does the work

    Every person On scale gets a proposed new pay. Pinned people are listed with their reason and left alone unless the policy says otherwise. Drifted people are shown with both numbers and a choice.

  4. Apply writes the contract, payroll takes it from there

    The same apply engine Pay Review uses: contract updated, 24-hour undo, letter generated. If the effective date is in the past, the difference is queued into the next pay run as a separate arrears line.

  5. Time moves people up the ladder

    The policy's step rule (for example, one step every 12 months after probation, manager confirms) creates a monthly queue of due step-ups on Home. Confirm all, confirm some, or skip with a reason.

Policies: rules you can read aloud

People → Pay → Policies · new

The hero: a policy is a paragraph, not a form. Every rule is a sentence with the changeable words highlighted. Click a highlighted word to change it. Under the paragraph, a live line says what the policy would do to real people today: "This policy puts 212 joiners this year at step 1 and moves 96 people one step next month. Show me." Nothing is saved until you have seen who it touches.

Retail store policy · Vietnam · applies to Payobook Retail and Payobook Logistics

New joiners start on step 1 of their job's grade, or a higher step if the recruiter records prior experience.

Pay follows the scale unless the contract is pinned. When the scale rises, pinned people keep their pay; people paid above their new step keep their pay; people below it move up to it.

A person moves up one step every 12 months after probation, when their manager confirms. At the top step, a rise becomes a one-time payment instead of a change to basic.

Amounts round to the nearest ₫10,000. No cell may sit below the Region I wage floor; a floor change proposes a revision automatically.

Scale changes are applied after HR review and Finance sign-off. Employees can see their own grade, step and next step date in the portal.

Tested just now: 1,138 people on scale, 41 pinned, 33 drifted. 96 step-ups due in October. Nothing below floor after the pending revision.

Every highlighted word is a choice with a short list behind it. The paragraph is also what the audit trail and the letters quote.

  • Scope. A policy applies to a country, a company, a division or a job family. The most specific one wins, and the screen says which policy governs any person you name.
  • Joiner rule. Step 1; or step by recorded experience; or step by the previous employer's pay matched to the nearest step; or "recruiter decides within the band".
  • Follow rule. What a revision does to people below, at and above their step, and to pinned people. Never a pay cut: the only options are keep, move up, or a one-time payment.
  • Step rule. Time in step, after probation, on anniversary or on a fixed date, automatic or manager-confirmed, blocked by an open performance plan if you choose.
  • Above the top. Freeze, or pay the rise as a one-time amount (the practice most companies use for people above the highest step).
  • Floors and rounding. Which country and region floor, the trained-worker uplift where an existing contract grants it, rounding unit, and what to do when the government moves the floor.
  • Approval and visibility. Who signs a revision, and what employees see of their own position. Nobody ever sees another person's pay here.

A revision, from the first edit to the letters

Starts from any scale edit · a full-screen stepped flow, never a pop-up

The hero: the cost is the real cost. Before anyone approves, the revision shows this month's payroll cost of the change computed through the real pay rules, per company and in the group currency: basic, the allowances that ride on basic, the employer's insurance and the tax effect. Not "1,212 people × 5%". Next to the total, a "show me one payslip" button opens any person's next payslip as it would print.

  1. Change the scale

    Edit cells, grow by a percentage, or apply a queued increase (a collective agreement's "+6% on 1 January 2027" sits here waiting). Pick the effective date. The floor check runs as you type.

  2. Who follows

    Three lists from the policy: moves (on scale), left alone (pinned, with reasons), needs a decision (drifted, with both numbers). Tick, untick, or bulk-decide. Add a person by name to include an exception.

  3. What it costs

    The true monthly cost, the yearly cost, and the arrears if the date is in the past, split by company in local money and rolled up in the group currency. Sends itself to the Decision Room as a plan line and to the Budget board as a forecast, so the numbers match everywhere.

  4. Approve

    The approval steps the policy names, as a timeline with a nudge button; each step is a task on the approver's Home. Rejection returns the revision with the reason on it.

  5. Apply

    Every contract updated in one go; the 24-hour undo; the next pay run prepared. Backdated? The difference for the closed months becomes an arrears line in the next run, visible on the payslip as its own row.

  6. Tell people

    Letters from the existing letter templates, batched; a contract appendix where the country requires one; the portal page updates the same minute.

The four existing screens that gain a touch

Hire screen

The wage box is filled before you reach it. "₫6,900,000 · Senior associate, step 1 of the Retail store scale." Slide "prior experience" and the step changes. Type a different number and a small line asks why, with a short list (offer match, retention, other), and the contract is pinned with that reason.

Contract drawer

One chip beside the wage: On scale · grade 3, step 2, next step 1 March 2027. Or Pinned · offer match, since 12 May. Or Drifted · scale says ₫7,300,000, paid ₫7,000,000, with a "bring to step" button that opens a Pay change pre-filled.

Home

Three new items in the needs-you dock when they apply: "96 step-ups due in October, confirm", "Revision awaiting your sign-off", and "The Region I wage floor rose on 1 January, 74 people are now below it". Each opens the right screen at the right row.

Employee portal

"Your pay on the scale." The person's own grade and step drawn as a small ladder with their cell marked, the amount, the date of their next step, and the last change with its letter. Only their own row, ever. Switched on per policy.

Also touched, lightly

  • Pay Review learns "steps" as a unit of guidance, so a review can propose "one step" instead of "4.1%", and shows the scale ticks on each row's band bar.
  • Pay changes gains "to grade and step" as the first way to describe a promotion; the amount follows.
  • Bands draw the scale's steps as ticks inside the band, so the two pictures agree.
  • Decision Room can start a plan from a draft revision, and the Budget board receives the forecast lines.
  • Command bar gets entries for Scales, Policies, New revision, Step-ups due, and "Where does this person sit on the scale".
  • Vietnamese for every new word, day one, tested the way the Pay area already is.

PART E

Wage floors: seven countries, dated, kept as data

A scale that can fall below the legal floor is a liability, and the government moves the floor every year or two. The design keeps floors as dated rows, per country and region, editable by you and shipped with the figures below. The scale editor, the hire screen and the contract drawer all check against the floor in force on the effective date.

Where the floor shows up

  • The scale editor: a dashed floor line, rose cells, the cost to fix.
  • The hire screen: a wage below floor is refused with the figure and the region named.
  • The contract drawer: a chip when a contract sits below the floor in force.
  • Home: a nudge when a new floor takes effect and people fall under it.

What the floor never does

  • It never changes a wage by itself. It proposes a revision; a person applies it.
  • It never guesses a region. A company or a work location names its region; if none is set, the check says so instead of passing.
  • It never silently stays stale. Each row carries its source and date, and a row older than 18 months shows an "is this still current" nudge.

PART G

What only Payobook will do

Every "New" in the matrix comes from one advantage the others do not have: the scale lives in the same product as the payroll that pays it. Here is each one, with the moment it pays off.

The true cost, before anyone signs

A 5% scale rise in Vietnam is not 5% of basic. It moves the insurance base, the allowances that ride on basic, overtime rates and the 13th month. Payobook prices the revision through the real pay rules, per company, in the group currency. A finance director signs a number that will match the pay run.

See the payslip before it exists

From any revision, open any person's next payslip as it would print. The question "what will Linh actually take home" is answered before approval, not after the run.

Three honest states on every contract

On scale, Pinned with a reason, Drifted. The reasons (offer match, retention, above the top, legacy) feed the Fairness page, which is exactly what the new European transparency rules ask employers to be able to explain.

A policy you can read to a labour inspector

The rules are sentences, the sentences are what the audit trail quotes, and the "tested just now" line proves the policy against real people before it is saved. No product in the matrix lets HR read its own pay policy aloud.

The floor is inside the scale

Seven countries, dated, with the region on the company or location. The government moves the floor; Home tells you who is now below it and what fixing it costs. Nobody else keeps the floor and the scale in the same picture.

Backdated means arrears, automatically

A revision effective 1 July applied in September puts the July and August difference on the September payslip as its own line, through the same "this run only" lane the product already has. No spreadsheet, no manual line.

The scale has a past

Drag the scrubber to any date and see the scale and the people as they were. Letters, arrears and audits read from the version in force on the day, never from today's table.

Start from what you pay

An empty scale offers itself from the median wage per job, rounded to your rule. The first version takes minutes, not a workshop. The same trick already fills empty Pay Bands.

Both ways of writing a scale

Amounts for a retail chain, coefficient times base rate for a state-linked manufacturer. One group can hold both, and compare them in one currency.

Steps that show up as work, not as a report

Due step-ups arrive on the manager's Home as a small list to confirm, respecting probation and any open performance plan. Time on the ladder becomes a monthly habit, not an annual clean-up.

PART H

Build plan: four phases, each usable on its own

Each phase ships to all databases, is tested against the demo Retail world, and leaves the product better even if the next phase never happens. Numbers are what you will see, not engineering size.

  1. Phase 1 · The ladder and the floor

    The Scales tab with the grid, versions, both writing styles, grade packages, import and proposal-from-wages; the wage floor table for seven countries with the checks in the scale editor, hire screen and contract drawer; the On scale / Pinned / Drifted chip. Result: you can define a scale, see who stands where, and the 2022 Vietnam floor is gone.

  2. Phase 2 · Joiners and the policy paragraph

    The Policies tab with the sentence composer and the "tested just now" line; the hire screen pre-filled and explained; Pay changes speaking in grades and steps; the employee portal page. Result: every new contract starts on the scale with a reason for any exception.

  3. Phase 3 · Revisions

    The full-screen revision flow: who follows, true payroll cost, payslip preview, approval, apply with undo, arrears, letters; Decision Room and Budget board receive the numbers; queued future increases. Result: a percentage rise for everyone on the scale is one flow with a real cost and a real undo.

  4. Phase 4 · Steps, group view and polish

    Time-based step-ups with the Home queue; scales side by side across countries; scale ticks in the band picture and in Pay Review; the command bar entries; the Vietnamese sweep; the closeout with what to decide next. Result: the ladder moves people by itself, with a person confirming.

Safety rails that hold in every phase

No pay is ever changed without a person pressing Apply, and every apply has a 24-hour undo. No wage is ever cut by a scale rule. Nothing a person can see is another person's pay. Every new word ships in Vietnamese. Every phase is rehearsed on a copy of the database before it touches the live one, as the last three programmes were.

For the engineers, collapsed
