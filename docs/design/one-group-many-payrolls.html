Do not switch off the old planning screens yet.

The new room replaces the picture, not the paperwork. Pay grades, the merit matrix, the approval chain that applies pay rises to contracts, and the table the Budget screen stores its numbers in all still live in the old module. I was wrong to imply a single Budget change was enough.

Choosing one payroll scheme in analytics is only half there.

You can slice by company, department and division. You cannot yet pick "Manufacturing payroll" as a scheme and compare it against "Retail payroll", because reports never record which scheme a number came from.

A consolidated view across countries would add dong to Singapore dollars.

Nothing in analytics converts currency today. This is the most important thing on this page. The fix already exists inside the Budget screen and needs to be lifted out.

QUESTION 1

What have you actually retired?

Nothing yet · and not everything can go

Right now nothing is retired. All seven old screens are still there, one press away, inside the fold called "Classic planning tools" at the bottom of the new room. That was deliberate. Having now read the old module properly, I am glad of it.

The old module is bigger than it looks: nineteen record types and about four and a half thousand lines of logic behind those seven doors. Some of it the Decision Room genuinely replaces. Some of it is not planning at all, it is paperwork and record-keeping, and a what-if simulator can never replace paperwork.

The hard blocker: your Budget screen lives inside the old module

The Budget screen you use does not have its own table. It stores every budget line, every uploaded budget and every actual it matches, inside a table that belongs to the old planning module. Uninstalling the module would delete that table and take your budget history with it.

So the order is: move the budget storage first, then decide about the rest. That is a real piece of work, not a switch.

What I would do instead

Keep the fold. Rename it so it stops looking like leftovers, and take the pieces worth keeping into the new world one at a time: the pay review workflow, pay grades and the merit table are worth keeping and worth redesigning. The old dashboard and the old scenario screens can go once the new room reads your payroll formulas. Section 7 has the order.

QUESTION 2

How is an employee tied to a payroll scheme?

Today: only through their payslips

This is the root of almost everything else you asked, so it is worth being blunt. There is no field on an employee, a contract or a job that says which payroll scheme pays them. The connection only appears once a payslip exists, because the payslip records the scheme that produced it.

In other words, the system knows how someone was paid last month. It does not hold a standing statement of how they are to be paid. For a single-scheme company like AB Mauri that never shows. For your Vietnam company, which runs fifteen schemes, it matters a great deal.

What exists today

  • The payslip carries the scheme. That is the only reliable record.
  • The Mapping screen lets you draw a picture of which department belongs to which scheme. It is stored, but no part of payroll reads it.
  • When a pay run is computed without being told the scheme, the system guesses: it copies the first payslip in the run, or falls back to the country, or to the first scheme it finds.

What that means in practice

  • A mixed run containing manufacturing and retail people can quietly pay everyone under one scheme.
  • A new joiner has no scheme until their first payslip exists.
  • Any report that asks "how many people are on the manufacturing payroll" has to work it out backwards from payslips.

How your demo company works around it, and why it is a good pattern

The Vietnam company has six divisions and two schemes each, an end-of-month payroll and a mid-month advance. Departments are organised the same way: a top-level department per division with its real departments underneath, so Manufacturing holds Assembly Line A and B, Quality Control, Maintenance and Warehouse. Payslips are then attached to the right scheme by division.

That is exactly the shape a real group needs. The only thing missing is making it official instead of a convention.

The counting trap you should know about

Most of these people are on two schemes in the same month: the mid-month advance and the end-of-month payroll. On your live system that happens 12,621 times. So "count people by scheme" double-counts unless the report is told to look at one kind of run only.

Any scheme filter we build has to answer "people paid at all" and "people on this scheme" as two different questions.

The fix

Make the department-to-scheme map real: the map you can already draw becomes the answer to "which scheme pays this person", it shows on the employee record, the pay run uses it to pick who is in the run, and reports read it. One person can still be on two schemes where that is genuine, an advance and a main run, because the map holds a scheme per kind of run.

QUESTION 3

Picking one scheme, or seeing them all, in analytics

Partly · division works, scheme does not

Your Analytics Explorer is built on a fast summary table that is rebuilt from finished pay runs. When it builds a row it does record which division and which kind of run the money came from, and which department and job the person was in. It does not record the scheme itself. So you can ask for Manufacturing, and you can ask for end-of-month runs, but you cannot ask for "the Manufacturing payroll scheme" and compare it with "the Retail payroll scheme" side by side.

Pay run resultsYes, per runNoShown separately
Government reportsBy countryNoOne company at a time
Decision RoomNoWhole companyOne company

The division route works today because of the convention described above, and it will keep working. But a division and a scheme are not the same thing. Two divisions can share a scheme, one division can have a special scheme for a plant, and a scheme can change year to year while the division name stays. If the CEO's question is "what does the manufacturing payroll cost us", the answer should follow the scheme.

The good news

The scheme is already written on every payslip and on every payslip line. Adding it to the summary tables is a small change to how they are built, not new record-keeping. Once it is there, the scheme becomes a filter and a grouping in the Explorer like any other: one scheme, several ticked, or all of them.

QUESTION 4

Different countries, different currencies

Not handled · and it can mislead you today

You are right to ask. Manufacturing in Vietnam and retail in Singapore is exactly the case the product is not ready for, and the failure is a quiet one rather than an error message.

Every payroll number is stored as a plain amount with no currency attached to it. Each screen decides on its own what symbol to draw. The Explorer, which is the one screen that can add several companies together, keeps no currency at all: if you gave it a Vietnamese company and a Singaporean one, it would add dong to Singapore dollars and show you one large meaningless number. Insights would do the same and then label the total with whichever company you happen to be viewing.

Say this plainly

Until this is fixed, do not use a group-wide total across two currencies. Compare countries side by side, each in its own money. The per-country and per-company numbers are sound; it is only the addition across them that is not.

What a proper answer looks like

The pattern already exists in your Budget screen, which does this correctly and can simply be lifted out and used everywhere:

  • A group currency. The parent company names the currency the board reads in, for example US dollars or dong.
  • Every stored number keeps its own currency. A dong figure stays a dong figure for ever.
  • Conversion happens when you look, not when it is stored, using the rate for that month, so last year's numbers do not move when today's rate does.
  • No invented rates. If there is no rate on file for a month, the screen says so and shows the figures separately rather than guessing. Your Budget screen already refuses in exactly this way.
  • The header always says which money you are looking at, and offers "show in each country's own money" as a single switch.

This also decides a smaller question you will hit immediately: the Decision Room's Vietnamese assumptions, the 23.5% employer contribution and the contribution cap, are correct for Vietnam and wrong for Singapore. A Singapore company opening the room today gets no cap and no tax ladder. Per-country assumption sets solve that, and they belong in the same piece of work.

QUESTION 5

Departments inside a payroll scheme

Works · but the link runs the other way

There are no departments inside a payroll scheme. A scheme is a calculation: a list of columns, the values that feed them and the formulas that turn them into pay. It has no idea what a department is.

Departments live on the person. Each employee sits in a department, the department sits under a parent, and that is where the structure lives. So "the departments in the manufacturing payroll" is answered from the other direction: the departments of the people that scheme pays. On your live system that reads back cleanly, five departments and just over a thousand people.

One detail worth knowing, because it is a quality point rather than a gap: when the Explorer builds its summary it uses the department a person was in at the end of that pay period, not the department they are in today. That is the correct behaviour for history. It means last March's numbers stay where they were even if somebody transferred in June.

The one thing missing is the standing statement in the other direction, "this department is paid by this scheme", which is the same fix as question 2. Draw that once and every screen can offer both directions: departments within a scheme, and the scheme behind a department.

QUESTION 6

Planning for one plant instead of the whole company

Possible today by team · not yet by scheme

The Decision Room opens on one company and splits it into teams, and a team is a top-level department. On your Vietnam company that gives exactly nine teams, and they are your divisions: Manufacturing, Retail, Logistics, Corporate Office, IT, Construction and so on.

So planning for manufacturing alone is already possible in a practical sense: move the levers on the Manufacturing team and leave the others alone. What you cannot do today is these three things.

  • Plan a scheme, rather than a team. If a scheme covers two divisions, or a division runs on two schemes, the team view no longer matches the payroll.
  • Use each scheme's own rules. The room applies one set of assumptions to the whole company. In reality a factory scheme and an office scheme can differ on overtime, shift pay and allowances, and those differences change the cost of hiring ten people.
  • Plan a group. The room deliberately opens on one company. A Vietnam plant and a Singapore shop cannot be planned on one screen, and until currency is solved they should not be.

What I propose on screen

  • A scope picker at the top: whole company, one division, or one payroll scheme.
  • The picker changes the roster, the assumptions and every number underneath it.
  • Plans remember their scope, so "Manufacturing 2027" and "Retail 2027" sit side by side in the comparison table.

Then, once currency is solved

  • A group view that adds the plans together in the board's currency.
  • Each country keeps its own rules underneath; only the presentation is shared.
  • The stage shows both: group total, and a strip per company.

There is a deeper prize here. The old planning module, for all its clumsiness, does one thing the new room does not: it costs a pay rise through your own payroll formulas, per person, per scheme, rather than through a general contribution rate. That is why it insists a scenario names a scheme. Bringing that engine under the new room is what would make plant-level planning genuinely accurate, and it is the reason I would not simply throw the old module away.

QUESTION 7

What I recommend, in order

Five pieces of work. Each is useful on its own, and each one makes the next easier. The first two are what unlock everything you asked about.

  1. Give every person a payroll scheme the foundation

    Turn the department-to-scheme map you can already draw into the real answer. It shows on the employee record, the pay run uses it to choose who is in the run and stops guessing, and a scheme can hold both a main run and an advance run. This removes the risk of a mixed run paying people under the wrong scheme, which is worth doing on its own merits.

  2. Make currency honest the safety fix

    A group currency on the parent company, every stored number keeping its own currency, conversion at the rate for that month, and a refusal to invent a rate. The mechanism exists in your Budget screen already. Until this lands, no screen should offer a total across two currencies.

  3. Put the scheme into analytics the answer to question 3

    Record the scheme on every summary row, then offer it as a filter and a grouping in the Explorer: one scheme, several, or all. Add a scheme comparison view, so Manufacturing and Retail payroll sit side by side per month with cost per head and overtime share.

  4. Give the Decision Room a scope picker the answer to question 6

    Whole company, one division, or one scheme, with that scheme's own rules and its own saved plans. Then a group view that adds several companies in the board's currency, once step 2 is in place.

  5. Absorb what is worth keeping from the old screens, then retire the rest the answer to question 1

    In order: move the budget storage out of the old module so your budget history is safe; rebuild the pay review, the approval chain and the limits as a proper Payobook workflow; keep pay grades and the merit table as reference data; bring the per-person costing engine under the new room. Only then does uninstalling the old module become a one-line decision.

If you only do one thing

Do step 2. A wrong number that looks right is worse than a missing one, and today a group total across Vietnam and Singapore is a wrong number that looks right.

For the engineers · the same answers with names and places

Scheme identity. hr.formula.config: company-scoped, unique code per company, required country_code, currency_id computed from the country code only, cycle_type regular/mid/end/full-final. No department, plant or branch field. pb_division is added by pb_demo, not by the engine, which is why the fact builder probes for its existence.

Employee to scheme. No formula_config_id on hr.employee, hr.contract or hr.version. hr.formula.scheme.assignment (config, department, optional domain) exists and is written only by the Formula Studio mapping canvas; nothing in the compute path reads it. The operative field is hr.payslip.formula_config_id, set explicitly by the import batch or resolved by _find_formula_config() down a five-rung ladder: sibling slip in the run, import batch, structure if unique, employee country, first active config. Rungs 1 and 5 are what make a mixed run dangerous. Proposal: make the assignment model authoritative, consult it in _find_formula_config above the country rung, expose it as a related field on the employee, and use it to build the run population in pb.payrun.wizard._eligible_employees.

Analytics dimensions. pb.fact.run / pb.fact.line / pb.fact.emp store company, month, quarter, cycle, division, basis, department, job, category, code. The builder already joins hr_formula_config but keeps only cycle_type and pb_division; adding config_id plus config_name to all three fact models and to _DIMENSIONS/_FILTERS in pb_explorer is the whole of step 3. Note hr.payslip.line already carries a stored formula_config_id and department_id from pb_hr_payroll_analytics, so the pivots can group by scheme today.

Currency. No _convert() or rate use in any analytics module. pb.budget.fx is the reference implementation: root-company presentation currency, rate_known() refusing an implicit 1.0, convert() returning (value, known), and an explanatory note when the rate is missing. res.company.presentation_currency_id is defined in pb_demo and should move to a platform module. Facts need a currency_id column; pb_explorer and pb_insights need a presentation-currency toggle and must refuse cross-currency sums when a rate is missing.

Known defects found while answering. hr_analytics_personnel_costs._get_payslips_for_period and hr_analytics_statutory_contrib search hr.payslip with no company_id filter despite carrying the field. pb_payruns.pb_payruns lists runs with search([]), no company filter, and prices every row in the active company's currency. hr.payslip.run has no company_id on this build, so pb_currency_id always falls back to the active company. payroll.analytics has no company_id at all and selects payslips by matching structure names as strings.

Legacy module. 19 models, ~4,400 lines. Two external writes: compensation_cycle.py writing hr.contract.wage, and the tagging wizard writing wfp_category onto hr.formula.rule. Contract-level grade_id, compa_ratio and range_penetration are stored columns other screens read. pb_budget inherits wfp.budget.actual and writes it from seven sites, so uninstall requires re-homing that model first. wfp.employer.cost.calculator is the piece worth porting: it costs a person through their own config's rules with a fixed-point evaluation pass and buckets the result by wfp_category.

Decision Room today. Single company chosen from env.companies as an allow-list; teams are root departments; department resolved as COALESCE(contract.department_id, version.department_id); currency is the company's functional currency; VN assumptions gated on the currency code being VND. Scope picker means adding a scheme/division dimension to _build_baseline and a scope field to pb.decision.plan and pb.decision.assumptions.
