Is it enough?

For the questions of structure, slicing, currency and planning: yes, and I have tried to be specific about the two places it stays partial, fiscal years in planning and the phone width of the hub frame. The one honest caveat is that "people in two places" is new ground for the payroll engine itself, not just the reports, and I have put it in its own phase with its own rehearsal so it cannot destabilise a pay run for anyone who never uses it.

PART B

The model: six ideas that carry everything

Most of what follows comes from adding six small, well-named things to the product and making every screen read them. None of them is exotic; together they are what a group needs.

1 · The group

A group record above the operating companies, with the board's currency, the exchange-rate policy, and the fiscal calendar on it. It is deliberately not the system's own "branch" feature, because a branch is forced to share its parent's currency, and Vietnam and Singapore cannot. One switch for the whole group.

2 · Divisions at group level

Retail is one record for the whole group. A department in any company attaches to it with a from-date. So "Retail" adds up across Vietnam and Singapore, and history stays put when a department moves.

3 · The scheme map

The one place that says "this department, for this kind of run, is paid by this scheme". Employees inherit it; the pay run reads it; exceptions are listed, never hidden.

4 · The person

One identity per human being across companies, holding the things that do not change when they move: name, national ID, tax number, start date with the group. Employments hang off it.

5 · Work segments

The days a person spends in an entity, division and scheme, with a full-time equivalent. A whole month is one segment. A joiner, a transfer, or a split month is two.

6 · Facts that remember

Every summary row keeps company, division, department, scheme and version, kind of run, currency, and the person, as at the end of its period. What you see is always reproducible.

How a person resolves to a scheme, in order

Today the pay run guesses down a ladder that ends in "the first scheme I can find". The new ladder ends in a named exception instead.

1 · A segment says soThis person, these days, this scheme. Wins always.
2 · Their department's map entryFor this kind of run. Walks up the department tree until it finds one.
3 · A rule on the map"Employment type is Intern" or "Job title contains Driver", when a department is not enough.
4 · The company's only schemeA single-scheme company behaves exactly as today. AB Mauri never notices any of this.
5 · NobodyNamed on the exceptions list, shown before the run starts, never paid under a guess.

PART D

Money across borders

One service, used by every screen, lifted out of the Budget screen where it already behaves correctly.

Stored

Every amount keeps the currency it was paid in. Nothing is stored converted, ever.

Converted

At view time, at the group's chosen rate for that month, remembering rate and date on the figure.

Refused

No rate on file means the parts are shown separately with a plain reason. A guessed rate is never shown.

Group settings

Settings → Group · new

The hero: your companies drawn as a tree under the group, each with its flag, currency and people count, and one card above them for the group currency and rate policy. Name the group, tick its companies, and it exists.

  • Rate policy: month average, month end, or day of payment. Shown on every converted number afterwards.
  • A rate coverage strip: which months have rates for which currency pairs, and a "fetch" button when a rate source is connected.
  • Divisions live here too: create Retail once, attach departments from any company, see the people count roll up.

PART E

People in two places

The hardest part of what you asked, designed so it also fixes something you did not ask about: pay for people who join or leave mid-month is not prorated today.

A work segment is the days a person spends in an entity, division and scheme, with a full-time equivalent. Every person has a standing segment that is simply "all month, my department, my scheme". Segments are only ever written for the exceptions: a mid-month joiner, a transfer, a secondment, a split month. Payroll then reads segments, not the person, so a month with two segments produces two payslips whose days add up to the month.

Each entity pays its own days

  • Vietnam pays 20 days under the Retail scheme in dong.
  • Singapore pays 10 days under the Logistics scheme in dollars.
  • Each with its own contributions and tax.
  • Cost lands where the work was done, no transfer needed.

Home pays, host is charged

  • Vietnam pays 30 days, one payslip, one set of rules.
  • A charge of one third moves the cost to Singapore in the reports.
  • The person stays one employee with one contract.
  • Right for short secondments and many expatriate arrangements.

Assignments

People → Employees → a person → Where they work · new

The hero: the month as a strip of days; drag to mark the ten days in Singapore, pick the entity and scheme, and the two payslips preview beneath it in their own currencies with the group total beside them, before you save anything.

  • One person, counted once: the group headcount and every division count use fractions that add to one.
  • The pay run shows split people with a chip, and previews both slips before computing.
  • The same screen handles a mid-month joiner, a transfer and part-time, because they are all segments.

PART F

Planning, by plant and for the group

Decision Room, with scope

People → Plan · existing, extended

The hero: a scope chip at the top of the stage that reads Retail scheme · Vietnam; click it and choose the group, a country, a company, a division or a scheme, and the roster, the rules and every number on the stage change to that scope in one motion.

  • Rule sets per country for the eight the engine knows, editable per scheme. Singapore stops inheriting Vietnam's contribution cap.
  • Group view: each entity's line in its own money, the group total in the board's currency, both on the same stage.
  • Full-time equivalents everywhere a split person exists.
  • A "true cost" lane that runs the plan through each person's actual scheme, for the months where accuracy matters more than speed.
  • Plan against actual: once a run closes, the stage draws the actual over the plan for that month.
  • Approve a plan, version it, and hand the approved rises to the pay review below.

PART G

The old planning screens, reborn

Seven old doors become three modern surfaces plus one thing that disappears into a screen you already have. Each is designed to the same bar as the Decision Room: a hero moment, no dead ends, plain language, motion with purpose, and keyboard and bulk ergonomics.

Pay Review

People → Pay Review · replaces Compensation Cycles, Planning Scenarios, Employee Forecasts

The hero: one grid of the people in this review, each row a sentence — Nguyen Thi Lan · Bread · ₫14.2M → ₫15.1M · +6.3% — with a budget meter at the top that fills as managers type and turns amber before it turns red. Type a percentage on a selected block of rows and every row updates, the meter moves, and any row that breaks a limit shows the limit in a chip on the row itself.

  • A cycle: scope (company, division or scheme), budget, deadline, approvers in order. Created from an approved Decision Room plan or from scratch.
  • Proposals: managers see their own people; inline editing, block selection, paste from a spreadsheet, undo.
  • Limits: maximum rise, band ceiling, division budget, minimum rating. Warn or block, with the reason on the row, never a pop-up.
  • Approvals: a horizontal timeline of who has signed and who is next, with a nudge button. Each step is a real task on the approver's Home.
  • Apply: a preview of every contract that will change, in local currency, with the exact date; apply writes the new salaries and keeps a one-click undo for 24 hours.
  • Trail: every state change, who and when, exportable, replacing the old audit table.
  • Money: each row in the person's currency; the budget meter in the cycle's currency; a group cycle shows both.

Pay Bands

People → Pay Bands · replaces Pay Grades and the band figures on contracts

The hero: every band drawn as a horizontal range with every person in it as a dot; drag a band's edge and the dots outside it light up with what it would cost to bring them in.

  • Bands per country and currency, with a job family and a level.
  • Each contract shows its band and its position in the band as a plain percentage, kept up to date.
  • Outliers, compression and inversion (a manager paid less than a report) surfaced as cards, not found by accident.

Merit Matrix

People → Pay Review → Rules · replaces Merit Matrix

The hero: a grid of performance across and position-in-band down, each cell a percentage you can type, with the total cost of applying it to the review's population recomputed live in the corner.

  • One matrix per country or per scheme; a review picks one.
  • Performance ratings come from the employee record; position in band from Pay Bands.

Component tagging, gone

Absorbed into the Mapping screen

Why it disappears: the payroll engine already knows what every column is — basic pay, allowance, deduction, employer cost — from the work done earlier this year on value kinds. The old tagging screen asked you to say it a second time. The planner and the review read the answer the engine already has.

What is kept from the old data

Pay grades, band positions on contracts, merit matrices, past compensation cycles with their approvals, and the budget table used by the Budget screen all migrate into the new records. Nothing you have entered is lost. When the last migration has run and every old door has a new one, the old module is uninstalled.

PART H

Seven phases, back to back

Each phase is a handover I write and a build the implementation agent does, tests and deploys, in the same order as the earlier programmes. The first two are the foundation everything else stands on.

  1. The group

    A group record with its member companies, group currency and rate policy, the shared conversion service lifted from the Budget screen, group-level divisions, and the Group settings screen. Nothing changes for any existing screen yet; the Budget screen switches to the shared service and behaves identically.

  2. Who is paid by what

    The scheme map becomes the truth: on the employee record as "Paid by", the exceptions list, and the pay run choosing its people by scheme with the count shown first and the scheme written on each payslip. Single-scheme companies behave exactly as today.

  3. Numbers that remember

    Scheme, version, kind of run, currency, division and person on every summary row. The Explorer's breadcrumb, scheme comparison, group currency switch and rate badges. The three report screens that forget the company are fixed or retired.

  4. Planning with scope

    The Decision Room's scope picker, country rule sets, group view, plan against actual, approvals and versions, and the true-cost lane carried over from the old engine.

  5. People in two places

    The person identity, work segments, day-based proration in the engine, both payment patterns, fractional headcount everywhere. Rehearsed on the clone with the split cases before it goes anywhere near a live run.

  6. Pay Review, Pay Bands, Merit Matrix

    The three new surfaces, the migration of every old record, the Budget screen's table re-homed, and the old module uninstalled.

  7. Visibility, Vietnamese, closeout

    Division and country visibility for every new screen, Vietnamese for every word, and a closeout that tells you what is live, what changed for your clients, and what to decide next.

What stays safe throughout

Every phase is rehearsed on the clone database and tested there before it touches a live company. Pay runs on a single-scheme company are unchanged until phase 2 and identical after it. Nothing is stored converted, so no currency work can corrupt a stored number. The old module stays installed and reachable until the last migration in phase 6 has been verified.

For the engineers · the same design with names and places

Invariants enforced by tests across all phases. No stored converted amount anywhere. Sum of segment days per person per month equals the calendar (or a named exception). Sum of segment FTE per person ≤ 1 unless flagged. Facts: sum over any grouping equals the sum over runs of the same filter. Headcount = distinct persons. Kind-of-run default = main. A company with exactly one active scheme and no map entries produces the identical run population before and after P2.
