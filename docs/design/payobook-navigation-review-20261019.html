Information architecture and product scope

A clearer way to navigate Payobook

The proposed architecture can be implemented, with substantial reuse. Adopt its business coverage, simplify its menus, and fund new screens only where they resolve a real workflow gap.

Recommendation ready for reviewLive demo inspectedSource code cross-checkedApplication implementation awaits a separate decisionSecond review (section 12): adopt with changesDelivered 2026-10-21: P1 rail, P2 regroup, P3a–d new views live on all databases (docs/handovers/NAV_CLOSEOUT.md)

Based on payobook.com, the supplied Payobook Menu-Sub Menu- document, and the local Odoo 19 codebase on branch 19.1. This report distinguishes observed behavior, code evidence and recommendations. All screen-preview figures are illustrative.

01 · Recommended decision

Keep the existing platform. Reorganize the work around clear business domains.

Use an expandable primary menu with one submenu level, then local tabs, filters and record actions. Make Hiring directly discoverable, place Lifecycle within People, group Travel and Expenses, and separate employee learning from Payobook help. Preserve the current calculation, approval, record and integration engines.

Proceed with navigation and reuse

Rename and regroup existing destinations, repair direct access, unify duplicate entry points, and preserve bookmarks and permission checks.

Add a focused set of workspaces

Manager work, payroll readiness, spend operations, cross-job hiring registers, skills evidence and policy ownership justify specific new views.

Stage larger product extensions

Manufacturing output analytics, corporate cards, booking services, general mentoring and succession require their own data and workflow scope.

I would not implement the Word document literally. “Create Job” is a button, “Draft Offers” is a filter, “Destination and Purpose” is part of a trip form, and “Gross Pay” is a result measure. Treating them all as menus would make a comprehensive capability list into an unnecessarily large navigation tree.

The recommended full architecture contains ten business entry points plus Settings and Help utilities. This deliberately exposes independent work such as Hiring and spend, while roles and enabled features control which entries an individual sees. A recruiter, employee or payroll operator should not receive the complete administrator map.

Scope of this decision: approve the information architecture first. Existing capabilities can be reorganized without commissioning every future feature. The “Later” items in this report are excluded from the proposed launch menu.

03

Design rationale and market inspiration

Borrow proven patterns, then fit them to Payobook’s existing architecture. Vendor documentation supports the patterns below; the proposed grouping is this review’s judgment, not a claim that one vendor’s menu is universally best.

Task and domain hubs

Workday documents hubs that combine a topic’s tasks, reports and overview. Apply that pattern to Hiring, Workforce and Payroll: one domain entry with clear destinations and contextual drilldowns.

Source: Workday Concept Hubs

Shared records and control services

Rippling describes shared workflows, reporting, permissions and approval management across applications. Payobook should similarly reuse employee, expense, approval and payroll records across its screens.

Source: Rippling platform

Employee and manager perspectives

BambooHR describes employees managing permitted personal information and requests, with manager oversight. Keep Payobook’s existing employee portal and add a coherent team view rather than showing employees an HR administrator’s menu.

Source: BambooHR employee self service

Recognizable labels and manageable depth

Hierarchy design balances breadth, depth and category meaning. For this product, use two sidebar levels; a third level may be local tabs on a complex workspace. Validate the labels with task-based tree testing instead of imposing an arbitrary menu count or a universal click limit.

Source: Nielsen Norman Group on flat and deep hierarchies

Accessible, predictable navigation

Pair consistent line icons with text, show the selected page, provide visible keyboard focus, and keep destinations available without hover. Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA; make touch targets comfortable and test reflow, keyboard use and translations.

Source: W3C WCAG 2.2

Menus answer where

A menu represents an object or stable workspace: Employees, Applications, Expense claims, Payroll inputs. It should remain meaningful as a record moves from draft to approved or paid.

Screens answer what next

Status filters, boards, calendars and form actions handle the work: Draft, Awaiting me, Create, Approve, Publish, Export. Every dashboard tile should open the exact underlying records that explain it.

Forms to reuse and forms to add

Reuse: employee and contract, pay-change proposal, requisition and job, application and interview, offer and joining checks, trip request, receipt/claim, goal/review, training assignment/test, HR ticket, filing proposal and approval configuration.

Add or extend only for the gaps: a non-pay employment-change request; skill requirements and assessments; policy version/publication and acknowledgement; obligation ownership/evidence; expense allocation and payment-reference fields where needed. Dashboard filters and list status tabs do not justify new forms or transaction tables.

Exact engineering estimates require validating installed module versions and walking these processes with their intended users. This review establishes a defensible implementation split without assigning unsupported completion percentages or development dates.

Data ownership and control boundaries

Navigation should expose the workflow without creating a second source of truth. A new dashboard can read several domains; it must not bypass their approval, accounting or access rules.

1 · EstablishEmployee, employment, contract and pay-group records
2 · CollectTime, overtime, variable pay and eligible expense inputs
3 · ValidateSource checks, approvals, period scope and input snapshot
4 · RunCalculation, variance review and authorized run approval
5 · DeliverPayslip publication, payment reconciliation and filing evidence
InformationCanonical ownerRequired handover rule
Person and employmentPeople, with distinct employment/contract recordsKeep person identity separate from entity-specific employment. Effective dates determine which record applies.
Time and approved overtimeWorkforceSource approvals and handover versions are traceable. Backdated changes create an exception/reopen or adjustment path.
Cash awards and variable payApproved incentive/input recordsRecognition is not payment. Link the approved monetary award to its run and reject duplicate inclusion.
Claims and reimbursementsExpense claim and accounting recordsAuthorization, posting and payment are distinct. If a claim is routed into payroll, retain a unique source link and block duplicate AP payment.
Results and payslipsPayroll run and calculation enginePersist the calculation/input version and approval history. Reopening after approval has an audited, permission-controlled path.
Filing completionFiling record with evidenceGenerated / approved / submitted / accepted cannot collapse into one green status.
Qualifications and certificatesSkills assessment plus shared document evidenceStore evidence once and reference it from training, employee profile and eligibility checks.
Production outputVerified ERP/MES or controlled production registerUse external source IDs, corrections and reconciliations. Payobook owns the employment/time/cost links, not a second uncontrolled production ledger.

Common context

Carry company, country, location, pay group and period through navigation when relevant. Display currency and timezone explicitly. A global “October” filter must not silently select one run for every company, country or off-cycle payment.

Keep list filters, selection, breadcrumbs and back navigation when opening and closing a record. Search uses the same destination metadata and permissions as the menu.

Authorization beyond the menu

Reuse role, company, team and feature checks on the server. Check list reads, counts, exports, drilldowns and direct URLs. Hiding a submenu does not protect records.

Confidential HR cases, candidate data, compensation and coaching need independent field/record access. Shared tasks and aggregate dashboards must not expand access.

Manufacturing metrics need their own data contract

At minimum: site, line/operation, product or work order, good output, rejected/reworked output, assignment intervals, actual labour hours, effective standard time, downtime reason and verified source record. Define treatment of shared labour, subcontractors, overtime, rework and product mix.

Example definitions to agree: earned standard hours = sum of verified good quantity × applicable standard hours per unit; labour efficiency = earned standard hours ÷ actual labour hours within the same boundary; labour cost per good unit = attributable labour cost ÷ verified good units. Missing or incomparable inputs yield Not available, not a ranked employee score.

These are proposed measures, not an assertion that the current demo already supplies the required inputs. Reconcile the source before enabling a manufacturing dashboard.

09

Implementation path after design approval

This is feasible as an incremental product change. Avoid replacing the current hubs in one release: make the route and ownership model stable, then improve the workflows that need new screens.

First release

Clarify navigation

A1–A4. Establish the destination catalogue, role/feature visibility and labels. Expose Hiring; regroup Lifecycle, talent, training and spend; preserve existing screens.

  • Role homes and shared Inbox entry
  • Applications and offers registers
  • Payroll readiness scope and evidence
  • Old-route compatibility and search aliases
Second release

Complete daily operations

A5–A11. Build the new views and the limited processes that the agreed operating model requires.

  • Spend overview and reimbursement aging
  • Non-pay employment changes
  • Skills and document exceptions
  • Policies, obligations and service reporting
Separate investment

Add verified capabilities

A12. Enable only after the transaction, integration and ownership requirements have been accepted.

  • Manufacturing allocation and analytics
  • General mentoring and succession
  • Advanced job-learning paths
  • Card/booking feeds and broad report scheduling

Fit the current technical architecture

  1. Use stable destination keys. Map each submenu to an existing action plus lens/focus context. New views get their own stable route; translated labels never become identifiers.
  2. Extend the rail deliberately. The current sidebar records are essentially a section/item structure; an expandable child level needs a supported parent/destination relationship or a derived catalogue. It is a small architectural extension, not just a relabeling of every XML item.
  3. Reuse hub extension points. Move registrations for Hiring, Training, Goals and Growth plans to their new owners. Avoid circular module dependencies. Retain standalone actions and redirects for existing bookmarks and embedded links.
  4. Preserve route ownership. Existing model/tag matches are broad. Employee and contract screens used by several domains need an explicit source/back context; avoid competing active-menu claims and “last registration wins” highlighting.
  5. Separate deployment from business migration. Navigation changes must not change payroll amounts, existing approvals or employee master values. Backfill a new policy or skills model only from verified records, using repeatable migration scripts.
  6. Use feature gates and a staged cutover. An uninstalled module has no destination. A future capability is hidden at launch. A configured capability with no data has a meaningful empty state. Preserve existing role and tenant entitlements.

For an approved implementation, follow the repository completion policy: commit only relevant changes, re-check the latest combined branch state, deploy that state to every agreed environment/database, test the deployed version in Chrome, report commits/targets/results, and remove test-generated PNGs. Environment and database scope must be established before that future rollout.

Approval boundary for this report: choose whether to adopt the architecture and the first-release scope. No application code, menu records, permissions or database data have been changed for this assessment. The HTML is a review document, not a deployment of the proposed application.

Second review: a shorter rail, one submenu standard

An independent re-read of sections 01–11 on 20 October 2026, with the leading cloud HR suites as the yardstick. Verdict: adopt with changes. The business grouping is right. The way the menu is drawn, the number of top-level entries, and the length of the labels are not, and the first release is too large.

Verdict: adopt with changesBenchmarked against Workday, Oracle, SAP, UKG, Rippling, BambooHR, HiBobCurrent code checkedMock-ups only, no application change
12.1 · What changes

Keep the dark sidebar for the big areas. Give every area the same sticky icon rail underneath. Cut the top level to what a person actually uses.

Five changes to the 19 October proposal: (1) no expanding accordion inside the sidebar; the second level is a sticky icon rail inside each area, exactly as Workforce has today; (2) eleven top-level entries at most, and a role sees only its own; (3) one- or two-word rail labels with the full name in the page header; (4) keep the words people already know: Compliance, Learn, Ask HR; (5) ship the rail standard first, move things second, build new screens third.

12.2 · What the market leaders do, and what to take from each

Gartner’s 2025 Magic Quadrant for Cloud HCM Suites (1,000+ employees) names SAP, Oracle and UKG among the Leaders, with Workday the long-standing reference point. The patterns below are what their navigation actually does; the last column is the lesson for Payobook, not a request to copy anyone.

12.3 · The one submenu standard: a sticky icon rail in every area

Today three different second levels exist side by side. Workforce has its own narrow rail with an icon and a short capital label for each item, always visible. Every other area (Home, People, Pay Run, Lifecycle, Insights, Compliance, Learn) uses the shared hub rail: a 64 px strip of bare icons that widens to 224 px and shows long labels only while the mouse is over it; that hover state is what the second screenshot shows. Settings has a third style, a fixed 226 px list with the label beside the icon. The owner has chosen the Workforce style. The mock below applies it to every area with the revised grouping from 12.4. Click an area in the dark sidebar, then an item in the rail.

Rail rules (the standard)

  • Sticky. The rail is pinned under the area header and never scrolls away. The page content scrolls beside it.
  • 72 px wide. One column. A 20 px line icon above a label; nothing to the right of the icon.
  • Label: one word, two at most. Capitals, 10 px, tracked. It must fit two lines at 72 px in English and Vietnamese. The full name goes in the page header and the hover tip.
  • Ten items is the design limit. Twelve is the hard limit. Beyond that the rail scrolls inside itself; it never wraps to a second column and labels never shrink.
  • Selected = one soft tint pill behind icon and label in the brand colour. Hover = lighter tint. Nothing else is coloured, so a count badge stands out when it appears.
  • Counts only on queues. INBOX, APPROVALS, ASK HR, CLAIMS may show a small number. Nothing else carries a badge.
  • Order is stable. Overview or Today first, then daily work, then periodic work, then set-up. Never reorder by usage.
  • Keyboard and link. Up/Down moves, Enter opens, the selected item is marked for screen readers. The address bar carries the item so bookmarks and lessons deep-link to it.
  • Phones. Under 768 px the rail turns into a horizontal strip under the area header, scrollable sideways, same icons and labels.
  • Hidden, not greyed. An item a person cannot use, or a feature a company has not enabled, is not drawn.

Why this beats the accordion in section 04

The 19 October explorer puts the second level inside the dark sidebar as an expanding list under each area. That has four costs. It makes the sidebar long and pushes Settings off screen. It forces long labels because the list is 265 px wide. It hides the second level whenever another area is opened. And it means two visual systems: the sidebar accordion and the hubs that already have rails.

The icon rail keeps the sidebar to eleven short words, shows every item of the open area at once, survives scrolling, and reuses what Workforce already ships. It is also what Workday’s 2026 sidebar, Oracle Redwood and HiBob converge on.

Build note (verified in code on 20 October). The shared hub shell already draws a rail; only its styling differs from Workforce. Changing the shell’s rail template and styles to the label-under-icon form converts seven areas at once. Workforce keeps its own rail and already matches; it should be folded onto the shared shell afterwards so there is one component. Settings swaps its 226 px list for the same rail with seven grouped categories. No business screen changes.

Eleven top-level entries including Settings, in the three groups the sidebar already uses. A role sees only the rows it is allowed; the fullest view is the administrator’s. 12.6 · A smaller first release

P1 · Rail standard everywhere

One shared rail component in the hub shell; every area switches to it; labels shortened; sticky; phone strip; deep links. Looks-only change. Nothing moves between areas yet. Lessons and tours re-checked for the new labels.

Reuse: the Workforce rail. Risk: low. Verify: every hub in Chrome, both languages, console clean.

P2 · Regroup

Trips → Expenses; Hiring → its own area; Goals, Growth plans, Praise, Training → Talent; Joiners, Probation, Exits, Contracts → People; Announce → Home; Ask HR → Home; Journeys → Settings. Old names become aliases; old links redirect.

Risk: medium (routes, lessons, menus per database). Verify: tree test with the six role walkthroughs in section 11.

P3 · New views

Hiring APPLICANTS and OFFERS registers (A3); readiness blockers on the Run board (A4); Home TEAM for managers (A2). Then A5 spend operations.

Each is a separate decision with its own design handover.

Decisions needed before P1 starts: the three naming calls in 12.4; whether Learn stays in GROW (recommended yes); whether manager TEAM is in scope for P3. Nothing in P1 needs a decision beyond approving the rail standard.
