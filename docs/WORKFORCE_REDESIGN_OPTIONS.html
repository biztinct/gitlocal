Workforce, made one product.

A file-by-file audit of all 14 Workforce screens, a field study of Deputy, Zoho, Rippling, Connecteam, When I Work and Homebase — and three design directions, each mocked up below, with a clear recommendation. The finding in one line: you don't have a Workforce product — you have fourteen products that share a sidebar.

Part 1 · Deep study of what exists

Three generations of UI are wearing the same sidebar

Every one of the 14 submenus was traced from its sidebar record to the exact view, component and stylesheet that renders it. The section splits cleanly into three generations built years apart in product-time — and the seam runs exactly along module lines.

Exhibit B — the same data, sold six times

Cohesion isn't just visual. The section repeatedly rebuilds the same information with no awareness of its siblings — the classic symptom of screens accreted one at a time.

6 screens

…all render hr.attendance

Live Attendance, Timecards, Weekly Entry, Attendance Control, Workforce Dashboard and Driver Tracking each built a private read-model over the same punches — six queries, six layouts, zero links between them.

5 inboxes

…for one approval job

My Team is a unified queue over OT + trips + corrections + leave. Yet Overtime Desk, Business Trips, Attendance Control and Leave each ship their own queue for their own slice. Same records, five places to check.

3 editors

…for one config model

"Overtime Rules" is a 1,084-line bespoke dashboard editing hr.overtime.config. Overtime Desk renders the same records as a card gallery, opening the native form — a third UI. One model, three faces.

Exhibit C — navigation is a set of dead ends

Moving between related screens — the exact pain you named — is unsupported by design. 7 of 14 screens have no outbound navigation at all. The five legacy screens share a two-segment breadcrumb whose two links are byte-identical — both go to the same dashboard:

// attendance_live.js:309 — repeated verbatim in timecards, roster, OT rules, payroll report
goHome()          { this.actionService.doAction('pb_dashboard.action_pb_dashboard'); }
goFlowDashboard() { this.actionService.doAction('pb_dashboard.action_pb_dashboard'); }  // ← same target

Edges that should exist and don't

  • Live Attendance's "Late / Not started" cards are Attendance Control's exception queue — no link
  • Shift Roster ⇄ Timecards (planned vs actual) — no link either way
  • Weekly Entry submits OT that Overtime Desk approves — no link
  • My Team aggregates four cockpits — links to none of them
  • Roster's quick-create picks Shift Templates — no link to manage them

Context is thrown away at every hop

Each screen keeps its own department filter, its own week picker, its own search. Move from Roster to Timecards and you re-select the same department and the same week — every time.

Deep links that do exist escape to raw native forms with no way back, and Payroll Report — a payslip screen — sits in Workforce next to attendance boards.

Part 2 · Gap analysis

Eight gaps between this section and the best in class

Each gap pairs what the code audit found with what Deputy, Zoho, Rippling, Connecteam, When I Work or Homebase actually ship. These eight are the design brief — every option in Part 4 is scored against them.

GAP 01 · INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Fourteen flat menu items, zero hierarchy of importance

Payobook today14 sidebar entries at equal weight — config screens (Shift Templates, Overtime Rules) sit beside daily surfaces (Live Attendance), and a payslip report hides among them.
Best in classDeputy runs an entire WFM product on ~6 top items. Connecteam ships 3 hubs. Zoho People 5.0 scopes everything by audience: My Space / Team / Organization. Config never competes with daily work.
GAP 02 · COHESION

No shared page anatomy — every screen re-invents the page

Payobook todayThree UI generations, ~12 accent identities, 5 reds, 5 greens, 4 background whites. A user crossing two menus changes visual worlds.
Best in classZoho hosts ~15 services in one repeating service-tab anatomy. Rippling reads as one product across 20+ "apps": one brand color, strict semantic status palette, same header/filter/grid skeleton everywhere.
GAP 03 · CONTEXT

Department, week and person are re-chosen on every screen

Payobook todayEvery screen owns a private department filter and week picker; nothing follows you. 7 of 14 screens have no outbound navigation at all.
Best in classDeputy keeps location + date as persistent context shared by Schedule, Timesheets and People — switching surfaces keeps the same slice. Numeric tiles drill into pre-filtered lists rather than making you re-filter.
GAP 04 · APPROVAL MODEL

Five inboxes, no exception-first triage, no auto-approval

Payobook todayOT, trips, corrections and leave each ship a separate queue; My Team aggregates them but links to nothing. Every entry is reviewed by hand.
Best in classDeputy splits timesheets into "issues" vs "clean", auto-approves within a configurable variance of the schedule, and lets managers bulk-clear the clean set. Rippling filters to "only unapproved" and resolves from push notifications.
GAP 05 · THE TODAY SURFACE

Live Attendance shows; it doesn't triage

Payobook todayA passive 4-column board. "Late" and "Not started" are displayed but not actionable — the correction flow lives two menus away with no link.
Best in classDeputy's Today: on-shift / on-break / late / scheduled tiles that drill into filtered people lists. Connecteam adds a live map of punch locations. Homebase surfaces "who's late" by default on the manager dashboard.
GAP 06 · SCHEDULING INSTRUMENTATION

A roster without cost, coverage or compliance in the canvas

Payobook todayThe Deputy-inspired roster has open shifts, leave overlay and conflict icons — but no wage cost per day, no coverage lens, no ceiling/compliance warnings while building, and it never meets Timecards' actuals.
Best in classDeputy docks budgeted-vs-scheduled-vs-actual wages above each day column and refuses invalid pastes. Rippling flags jurisdiction rule breaches as you build. When I Work overlays required-vs-scheduled coverage.
GAP 07 · TIME ENTRY & RECONCILIATION

No single place to see or enter one person's week

Payobook todayTimecards (view-only Gantt), Weekly Entry (bulk grid), and Attendance Control (exceptions) are three unlinked screens. There is no per-employee week view with table entry, no scheduled-vs-actual reconciliation, and no lock state before payroll.
Best in classZoho shows one attendance dataset through four switchable lenses including a per-day tabular view with inline regularization. Connecteam approves then locks days so payroll can't drift. Homebase runs a "daily review" ritual of scheduled vs actual.
GAP 08 · LIFECYCLE & ACKNOWLEDGMENT

Work states exist in the models but not in the experience

Payobook todayDraft/published shift states exist, but there's no employee acknowledgment, no "all confirmed" signal, no close-the-week ritual, no sentiment loop.
Best in classWhen I Work shows a green badge when an employee confirmed all shifts. Deputy sorts confirmation-required posts first and captures anonymous shift-end sentiment (Shift Pulse) — the cheapest wow in the category.
Part 3 · Field notes

What the best products actually do

Condensed from vendor documentation and hands-on reviews, 2024–2026. Full pattern-by-pattern notes fed the options; these are the signatures worth remembering.

Deputy

The shift-work benchmark
  • Exception-first timesheets: "issues vs clean" split, tolerance-based auto-approval, one-click rounding, bulk approve
  • Stats panel in the scheduler: budget vs scheduled vs actual wages per day column; hover any hour for required-vs-scheduled staff
  • Refuse-on-paste: copying a shift re-validates leave, availability, fatigue and location before it lands
  • Leave approvals with context: live projected balance + who else is off in the window, inside the dialog
Steal: tolerance auto-approval + budget docked in the canvas

Zoho People / Shifts

Many modules, one grammar
  • Audience-scoped IA: My Space / Team / Organization tabs organize ~15 services
  • One dataset, four lenses: attendance as List / Calendar / Tabular / Overtime views on a single page
  • Regularization: employees request punch fixes through an approval flow instead of managers editing raw data
Steal: lens-switching over one dataset — never four pages

Connecteam

Mobile-first, hub-based
  • Three hubs (Operations / Communications / HR) hold the entire product
  • Today view: who's in, on which job, hours so far, plus a live map of punch locations
  • Approve-then-lock: locked days can't drift before payroll; separate approve vs reopen permissions
  • Full admin from a phone — approvals, schedules, reports
Steal: the punch map + the day-lock state machine

Rippling

The design-excellence reference
  • Unified employee graph: scheduling reads pay, certifications, leave and location natively — rules need no integration
  • Compliance while you build: jurisdiction rest/break/OT warnings appear as the schedule is drafted, not after
  • Push triage: missed punch / OT-risk notifications resolvable in one tap
Steal: validation at the moment of action, never in a report

When I Work

Scheduling ergonomics
  • Coverage view: required-vs-scheduled staffing as an overlay lens on the schedule
  • Shift confirmation: employees ack shifts; manager sees a green all-confirmed badge per person
  • Labor budget bar docked at the bottom of the scheduler with sales & labor-% targets
Steal: read-receipts for schedules

Homebase

Small-business polish
  • Late by default: the manager view leads with who's late, not who's fine
  • Daily review ritual: a scheduled-vs-actual reconciliation screen after each day closes
  • Forecast-fed scheduling: sales + weather feed labor-cost targets while building
Steal: the end-of-day reconciliation moment

Seven laws of cohesion these products obey

Independent of features, this is why those products feel like one thing while Workforce feels like fourteen:

One brand color, strict semantic status palette. Brand marks identity; red/amber/green mark state; nothing else carries color.
One page anatomy, repeated: header → persistent context → lens switcher → exception ribbon → content. Users learn it once.
Context follows the user. Department, week and person survive every navigation. Tiles drill into pre-filtered lists.
Lenses, not pages. One dataset with switchable views beats four screens over the same table.
Exceptions first. The default manager job is clearing a short flagged list, not scanning everything.
Validate in the canvas. Warnings appear on the cell being edited, at the moment of action — never in a later report.
Every record is a door. Any avatar, any shift, any number opens its detail in place — no dead ends, ever.
Part 4 · Three design directions

Three ways to make it one product

All three share the same non-negotiable foundation — one token system, Lucide icons, zero gradients, the pbim indigo — and differ in how radically they restructure the experience. They are staged deliberately: B contains A, and C's engine can power either.

OPTION ASix Hubs — consolidate & unifyEvolutionary · lowest risk

Thesis: the content is mostly right; the packaging is wrong. Collapse 14 menu items into 6 hubs + Trips, each hub built on one shared page anatomy with lens tabs over a single dataset — Zoho's lens pattern wearing Payobook's design system. Your seven modern cockpits survive as lenses; the seven legacy screens are retired, rebuilt or folded in.

The shared page anatomy — learned once, true everywhere

1Hub header — name, primary action. One solid-indigo button per page, everything else outline/ghost.
2Persistent context bar — department · week · person search. Synced across all hubs: switch hubs, keep the slice.
3Lens tabs — switchable views over one dataset (Zoho pattern). Counts on tabs that need attention.
4Exception ribbon — what needs review, before the content (Deputy pattern). Dismissed when clear.
5Content + detail drawer — every avatar and number opens detail in a right drawer. No page-jump, no lost context.
Why this is the safe bet: the sidebar is data-driven (pb.sidebar.item records), so the IA change is data, not code. Only three surfaces are truly rebuilt: the Today board, the Timeline lens, and the Schedule chrome. Everything ships hub-by-hub with no big-bang cutover.

Thesis: stop navigating; stay in the room. Workforce becomes a single full-height workspace. OPTION CThe Manager's Day — four verbs, one loopOpinionated · process-led

Thesis: organize by the job, not the data. A workforce manager's week is a loop: Today (who's here, fix what's wrong) → Plan (roster the coming week against coverage and budget) → Approve (clear the exception list) → Close (reconcile, lock, hand to payroll). Option C makes those four verbs the entire IA and pours the engineering into the workflow engine underneath: tolerance-based auto-approval, day-lock states, employee acknowledgment, shift-end pulse. Records and configs move behind ⌘K and a settings page.

Recommendation

Build B — Mission Control — staged through A, powered by C's engine.

The three options aren't really rivals: A is B's first milestone, and C's engine is what makes either feel intelligent. Consolidating to hubs (A) is work you must do under any future — it retires all Gen 0/1 debt and unifies the tokens. The Mission Control shell (B) is the only option that solves the navigation problem structurally instead of politely, and it's the one that produces the "how is this an Odoo app?" reaction.
