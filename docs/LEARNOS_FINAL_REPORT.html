What you reported, and what it actually was

You saw a brand-new tenant, abm.payobook.com, showing 1,230 employees and ₫28.0M of monthly payroll on a database with nothing in it — and asked whether it had been seeded by mistake.

The golden template was clean. The dashboard was lying.

Those four numbers were hardcoded sample constants living inside the old analytics module. Whenever a database had no payslips, the dashboard quietly fell back to them and presented invented figures as if they were real. Nothing had been seeded — the tenant was genuinely empty, and the screen was fiction. Two sibling surfaces did the same thing, and one of them created real employee and department records to make its demo look convincing.

That became the program's first rule: no surface may ever show a number it did not measure. Sample data is now only possible when the demo module is installed, and it is always labelled.

What a new customer experiences now

The same journey, before this program and after it.

Before
  • Dashboard invents 1,230 staff and ₫28.0M of payroll
  • A clunky "Guided tours" popup with six list items
  • Tour copy written for the demo world — "your 12 division configs"
  • Lessons and answers loaded from database records that a tenant might not have
  • Language written for people who already know payroll
  • No safe place to practise; the only way to learn was to do it for real
After
  • Honest zeros, plus a five-step checklist from empty database to first real payslip
  • One walkthrough plays three ways: Watch, Try, Do
  • Every technical word has a tap-for-plain-English card
  • All content is a static file — identical on any tenant, nothing to corrupt
  • Every sentence rewritten for a beginner, in English and Vietnamese
  • A watermarked practice company you can wander freely

Watch

The system drives. A spotlight moves across the real screens and explains each control as it goes.

Try

You drive, on a fake company. You click and type for real; a wrong click gets a gentle nudge, never a failure.

Do

You drive on your own data, and the guide stops before anything that writes: "You press it — I'll wait."

Waiting on you

Three decisions only you can make. Nothing is broken while they wait.

1. Nobody can reach the Companion settings screen

The screen that turns the AI features on is restricted to a "learning author" group — and that group currently has zero members on the live server. Until someone is added to it, the switches can only be flipped by a database command.

What I need from you: tell me who should hold it (yourself, presumably) and I'll grant it.

2. Four features are built, tested, live — and switched off

Deliberately, per tenant: the AI answer composer, Continue where you left off, the progress rings and streak, and voice input. They were each validated on the live server and then turned back off, because switching on an AI feature for real customers is your call, not mine.

What I need from you: say the word and I'll enable any combination, on the apex only or everywhere.

What the privacy protection does and does not cover

Written down inside the code itself, so the next person to touch it cannot miss it.

Protected

  • Employee names are replaced with placeholders before any question leaves the server, and put back only in what you read.
  • Emails, phone numbers, record ids and money amounts are stripped from free text.
  • The tutorial assistant only ever reads its own teaching material — never a payroll record.
  • "Continue where you left off" and the progress rings are computed on your own server; nothing leaves.
  • Spoken replies are read aloud by your own browser — the answer is never posted to a speech service.

Known limits, stated on purpose

  • On three of four question types, your typed question goes out as you wrote it — scrubbing a live question would ruin the answer.
  • Voice sends raw audio to a speech service. Names cannot be removed from a recording — the consent card says exactly that.
  • A salary typed with no separators (15000000) slips past the amount filter.
  • A half-accented spelling of a name in free text may not be caught.

The three that would have hurt

  • Two employees with near-identical names — Hùng and Hưng — collapsed into one entry in the name-protection map, so the second person's real name would have gone out in full. Caught by a reviewer's own test case, not by ours.
  • The PDF report's AI narratives carried alert summaries that already had real names put back into them — protected everywhere except the one place they were re-sent.
  • Tenant provisioning could have been aborted entirely by the new currency setting, on any tenant that had ever posted an accounting entry.

Two silent rollbacks on the live server

Unrelated to this work, and invisible to a normal upgrade: the server's files had been reverted to older versions while each database still believed it was current. One of them had disabled the sidebar's collapse; the other could let a pay run skip the HR approval tier. Both restored. Every deploy now checks disk against database.

A test suite that had never actually run

The thirteen tests guarding the practice sandbox were silently erroring out on a naming collision — reported as environment noise, never as failure. The rehearsal step caught it, and the sandbox's containment was proven properly before it shipped.
